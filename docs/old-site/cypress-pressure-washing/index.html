What Cypress homes need cleaned

Cypress grew up along US-290 as a string of master-planned communities, from Coles Crossing and Cypress Creek Lakes to Towne Lake, Fairfield and Bridgeland. The homes are mostly newer brick and stone with fiber-cement siding, wide concrete driveways and plenty of walks and patios. That makes most Cypress cleaning jobs a mix of three things: concrete, walls and fences.

HOA letters

A lot of Cypress pressure washing starts with a letter from the HOA about a dark driveway, mildew on the siding, or a fence that has gone green. If that is you, the fix is usually one visit. Tell the company what the letter asks for, and ask them to photograph the work when they finish, so you have something to send back.

Driveways, walls and fences

  • Driveways and walks: cleaned with a rotary surface cleaner for an even finish, with oil and rust stains treated first.
  • Brick, stone and siding: soft washed at low pressure with a cleaning solution. Houston humidity grows mildew on the north side of Cypress homes year-round, and it comes off without blasting the mortar.
  • Fences: cleaned at low pressure with a wood cleaner so the boards do not end up fuzzy. See fence cleaning.
  • Roofs: soft washed only. See roof cleaning.

Pollen season

Every spring, oak and pine pollen coats Cypress driveways, cars and patio furniture in yellow. It washes off, but it comes back until the season ends. Most homeowners wait until late spring to wash the house and flatwork so it stays clean longer.
