## The Woodlands

### Facts
- The Woodlands opened on October 19, 1974, as a master-planned community created by George P. Mitchell. (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/Explore-More/George-Mitchells-Vision ; https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/woodlands-tx)
- It sits mostly in Montgomery County, with part in Harris County. It had 114,436 residents at the 2020 census. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodlands,_Texas)
- There are nine villages: Grogan's Mill, Panther Creek, Cochran's Crossing, Indian Springs, Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge, College Park, Carlton Woods and Creekside Park. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodlands,_Texas)
- Governance: The Woodlands is a township, not a city. Its community standards are enforced through covenants rather than ordinances. The original covenant holders were The Woodlands Community Association, The Woodlands Association and The Woodlands Commercial Owners Association, and in 2010 covenant administration moved to The Woodlands Township. (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/Departments/Covenant-Administration ; https://www.woodlandsonline.com/npps/story.cfm?nppage=82029)
- Each village has a Residential Design Review Committee (RDRC) of at least three resident volunteers, elected each year, that meets monthly. Exterior changes to a lot with an existing home need prior written RDRC approval. That includes paint color changes, decks, fences and paving. (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/Departments/Covenant-Administration ; https://www.thewoodlandstx.com/government/home-improvement-guidelines.php)
- Repainting or re-staining in a color that is already approved does not need approval, but a change in exterior color or material does. (confirm) (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf)
- Fences: wood fencing must be allowed to weather naturally. Any paint, solid stain or tinted sealant must be a muted shade and approved first. Transparent and semi-transparent stains listed in Appendix C are pre-approved. (confirm) (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf)
- Residents can report possible covenant violations through the Township's 311 "Report a Concern" portal. (Source: https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/Departments/Covenant-Administration/Compliance-Violations)
- Tree cover: the forest has a canopy of pine (loblolly and slash) and oak (red and water), a midstory of magnolia, dogwood and crepe myrtle, and an understory of wax myrtle and yaupon. (Source: https://www.thewoodlandsparks.com/files/sharedassets/parks/v/2/documents/parks-amp-pathways/woodlands-township-forestry-management-plan-2025.pdf ; https://communityimpact.com/the-woodlands/environment/maintaining-tree-canopy-sustainability-part-of-the-woodlands-50-year-history/)
- A 2004 forest analysis counted about 20,900 acres of canopy. After the 2010 to 2011 drought, about 18,500 acres remained. In 2024 the Township raised its reforestation budget to $1 million. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/the-woodlands/government/2025/04/22/the-woodlands-tackles-tree-loss-due-to-invasive-species-and-climate-patterns/ ; https://communityimpact.com/the-woodlands/environment/maintaining-tree-canopy-sustainability-part-of-the-woodlands-50-year-history/)
- Water: Woodlands Water customers in the Montgomery County part follow a permanent Defined Irrigation Schedule that allows automatic sprinklers two nights a week. Odd addresses water Tuesday night into Wednesday and Friday night into Saturday. Even addresses water Wednesday night into Thursday and Saturday night into Sunday, 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. Hose watering with a nozzle and drip irrigation are exempt. (Source: https://woodlandswater.org/defined-irrigation-schedule/)
- Water bills carry a Surface Water Conversion fee that passes through to the San Jacinto River Authority (SJRA) for treated surface water from Lake Conroe. (confirm) (Source: https://woodlandswater.org/qa-extreme-weather-and-water-in-the-woodlands/)
- Woodlands Water publishes stormwater guidance on keeping debris, chemicals and waste out of storm drains, which lead to local waterways. (Source: https://woodlandswater.org/stormdrain-waste/ ; https://woodlandswater.org/stormwater-contaminants/)
- Hurricane Harvey (2017) flooded homes along Spring Creek and Panther Branch. More than 300 homes flooded in the Timarron / Timarron Lakes area. (Source: https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/woodlands/news/article/Time-lapse-videos-show-how-Harvey-flooded-parts-12928215.php ; https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/woodlands/news/article/Woodlands-residents-sue-companies-over-design-of-12956892.php)

### Angles for a unique page
1. **Covenants, not city code.** Exterior standards are set by the Township and the village RDRCs. Re-staining in an approved color needs no approval, but a new fence color does. A page can explain how to ask the company about stain or sealer choices before cleaning a fence or deck.
2. **Living under pines and oaks.** Heavy shade, pine needles in gutters and on roofs, and spring pollen on siding and decks mean north-facing walls and shaded driveways stay damp. That makes a case for soft washing siding and roofs and for checking gutters.
3. **Wood fences that "weather naturally."** The standards prefer wood fences left to weather. Cleaning gray, mildewed fence boards without scarring them comes down to gentle pressure and the right products. Explain what a good company should do, with no settings.
4. **Wash water and the forest waterways.** Lake Woodlands, Spring Creek, Panther Branch and the many drainage channels mean runoff matters. Ask how the company handles rinse water near storm drains.
5. **Irrigation-schedule overspray.** Sprinklers running on a fixed schedule can leave water spots and mildew streaks on low brick and siding.

## Katy

### Facts
- Katy sits where Harris, Waller and Fort Bend counties meet, about 25 miles west of Houston along I-10. It was first known as Cane Island, and the Katy post office opened in 1896. (Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/katy-tx)
- Rice farming came to Katy in 1901. The first concrete rice dryers in Texas were built there in 1944. (Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/katy-tx)
- The City of Katy was incorporated in 1945. Its population was 21,894 at the 2020 census. (Source: https://www.cityofkaty.com/about ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy,_Texas)
- Note for copy: much of what people call "Katy" (Cinco Ranch, Kelliwood, Canyon Gate, Grand Lakes) lies outside the city limits. Cinco Ranch is a census-designated place in Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction, in Fort Bend and Harris counties, with 16,899 residents in 2020. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinco_Ranch,_Texas)
- In February 1984, Cinco Ranch Venture (U.S. Home, the Mischer Corporation and American General) bought Cinco Ranch for a 5,000-acre master-planned development. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinco_Ranch,_Texas)
- Katy Mills mall opened on October 28, 1999. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Mills)
- Water source: the City of Katy runs six groundwater plants, two in Harris County, three in Waller County and one in Fort Bend County, with combined storage of 8,750,000 gallons. (confirm) (Source: https://www.cityofkaty.com/government/water-conservation)
- The City's Drought Contingency Plan has stages from Mild to Emergency. At Stage 2 the city encourages less water use for power washing buildings, sidewalks, walkways, driveways, parking lots and other hard surfaces. At Stage 3, hosing paved areas, buildings, windows and hard surfaces is prohibited. (confirm exact stage wording) (Source: https://ecode360.com/38862596)
- Katy has declared Stage 2 and Stage 3 drought restrictions in past summers, for example 2022 and 2023. (Source: https://www.houstonchronicle.com/neighborhood/katy/article/stage-3-drought-restrictions-17359015.php ; https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2023/07/22/city-of-katy-under-stage-2-drought-contingency-plan-as-heat-creates-high-water-demand/)
- Stormwater: the City has a Stormwater program in Public Works. Residents report illicit discharges to the Stormwater Technician at (281) 391-4876. City stormwater provisions carry fines of up to $2,000 per offense, and each day counts as a separate offense. (confirm whether the fine clause covers all discharges or only the construction article) (Source: https://www.cityofkaty.com/government/city-departments/public-works/storm-water ; https://ecode360.com/38863692)
- Hurricane Harvey (2017): water pooled behind Barker Reservoir and entered thousands of homes in parts of Cinco Ranch, Kelliwood and Canyon Gate. In 2019 a federal judge found the government liable for flooding upstream of the Addicks and Barker reservoirs. (Source: https://www.click2houston.com/news/2018/08/28/families-in-cinco-ranch-reflect-one-year-after-harvey/ ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/katy/government/2019/12/18/judge-finds-government-liable-for-flooding-property-owners-upstream-of-addicks-barker-reservoirs-after-hurricane-harvey/)
- The Addicks and Barker dams were built in the 1940s to control flooding on Buffalo Bayou. (Source: https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/shows/below-the-waterlines/2022/08/09/429488/five-years-after-harvey-a-solution-to-the-flood-threat-from-the-addicks-and-barker-reservoirs-remains-a-long-way-off/)
- HOA: the Cinco Ranch associations enforce deed restrictions through the Declaration of Protective Covenants, which lets the association impose sanctions for violations. Cinco Ranch II runs a fence maintenance program for association-owned fencing that includes removing ivy and cutting back vegetation from fences. (confirm) (Source: https://www.mycincoranch.com/faq.php ; https://www.mycincoranch.com/blog.php?view=blog&id=86 ; https://www.cincoranch.life/faq.php)

### Angles for a unique page
1. **Three counties, many rulebooks.** An address can be in the City of Katy, in Cinco Ranch (Houston ETJ) or in a MUD in unincorporated Harris, Fort Bend or Waller county. Which drought and stormwater rules apply depends on where you live. Tell readers to check their water provider before a summer cleaning.
2. **Drought stages and power washing.** The City's own drought plan names power washing of driveways and buildings. At Stage 3, hosing hard surfaces is prohibited. A good company should know the current stage and plan around it.
3. **Prairie wind and dust.** Flat former rice prairie means dust, pollen and red-clay soil splash on lower walls and fences. Siding and brick on new tract homes collect grime at the bottom first.
4. **After the floodwater.** Homes near Barker and Addicks that went through Harvey may still have water lines or staining on brick, fences and driveways. Mention this carefully, as context only, with no claims about results.
5. **Large master-planned HOAs.** Cinco Ranch and similar communities enforce fence and landscape standards. Cleaning a fence and cutting back vines often happen together.

## Sugar Land

### Facts
- Sugar Land is a city in northeastern Fort Bend County, on Oyster Creek and US 90A, east of the Brazos River. Matthew Williams set up a commercial sugar mill on Oyster Creek in 1843, on the site that became Imperial Sugar. (Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/sugar-land-tx)
- The city incorporated in 1959, a year after Imperial Sugar and Sugarland Properties began selling homes, businesses and land. (Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/sugar-land-tx)
- The population was 111,026 at the 2020 census. Growth included the annexation of Greatwood and New Territory in December 2017. (Source: https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/213/Population-History ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_Land,_Texas)
- Master-planned communities include First Colony, Greatwood, New Territory, Telfair, Sugar Creek, River Park and Imperial. Development of First Colony began in 1976 on former sugarcane land. Telfair land was annexed in July 2004. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_Land,_Texas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Colony ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telfair,_Sugar_Land,_Texas)
- HOA, a key fact: the First Colony Community Services Association lists "mildew/mold/stains" on the home, fence or driveway/walkway among its "Top Ten" most often cited deed restriction violations. (Source: https://www.firstcolony.org/resources/faqs/)
- First Colony's enforcement uses three letters, each with a 30-day cure period: a courtesy notice, a violation notice, then a certified letter. A policy effective September 1, 2023, raised fines from $200 to $400 per month and limits fine waivers to one every 12 months. (Source: https://www.firstcolony.org/latest-news/fccsa-deed-restriction-enforcement-process/ ; https://www.firstcolony.org/latest-news/residential-deed-restriction-policy-changes/)
- First Colony requires prior approval from the Modifications or Covenants Committee for all exterior changes. (Source: https://www.firstcolony.org/community-standards/)
- City code: residents must keep exterior materials, including brick, wood, siding, roofs, windows and doors, free of holes and breaks, and all wood surfaces painted, protected from the elements and in good condition. "Exterior structure deterioration" is a listed code violation. Sugar Land has adopted the 2024 International Codes, including the Property Maintenance Code. (confirm) (Source: https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/1420/Violations ; https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/450/Code-Enforcement ; https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/2463/Building-Codes)
- Stormwater: a 2010 ordinance, Code Chapter 11 "Stormwater Quality Management and Discharge Control", bans illicit discharges into the city storm drain system. Water containing detergents or chemicals is listed as prohibited. Individual residential car wash water is listed as allowed. (Source: https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/2214/Stormwater-Quality ; https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/2218/Stormwater-Management-Program)
- The Drought Contingency Plan defines non-essential water use to include using water to wash down sidewalks, walkways, driveways, parking lots, tennis courts or other hard-surfaced areas. (Source: https://sugarlandtx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/29860/2022-Retail-and-Wholesale-Drought-Contigency-Plan)
- Water source: the Fort Bend Subsidence District requires groundwater cuts. Sugar Land opened a 10.85 MGD surface water treatment plant in 2013 near Voss and Burney roads (Gannoway Lake area) and runs a Groundwater Reduction Plan. (Source: https://www.sugarlandtx.gov/331/Groundwater-Reduction-Program ; https://fbindependent.com/sugar-land-makes-history-with-largest-capital-improvement-project-p6745-91.htm)
- Levees: nine levee improvement districts border the Brazos River, protecting about 80,000 residents. (confirm the figure on the city PDF) (Source: https://sugarlandtx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/913/Levees-Protect-Sugar-Land?bidId=)
- Hurricane Harvey (2017): the Brazos crested above 55 feet. No river water entered Fort Bend County LID 2, which protects about 5,000 acres of First Colony. Still, more than 31 inches of rain fell inside the levee and over 200 homes there flooded from rainfall. (Source: https://www.fbclid2.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/fbclid2_newsletter_2017-fall.pdf ; https://damsafety.org/content/hurrican-harvey-analyzing-and-preventing-floods-inside-a-levee)

### Angles for a unique page
1. **Mildew is a named HOA violation.** First Colony lists mildew, mold and stains on homes, fences and driveways in its Top Ten violations, with a three-letter, 30-day process and monthly fines. A page can explain how to clear a notice in time. This is the strongest local hook for Sugar Land.
2. **City code also covers wood.** Wood surfaces must be painted and protected. Cleaning before repainting a fence or trim is a practical, code-linked reason to wash.
3. **Detergent runoff is regulated.** Chapter 11 names water with detergents or chemicals as a prohibited discharge. A good company should control where soft-wash rinse water goes, especially near Oyster Creek and the many lakes inside the levees.
4. **Hard-surface washing in a drought.** Washing down driveways and sidewalks counts as non-essential use under the drought plan, so timing matters in a dry summer.
5. **Lakes, levees and humidity.** Neighborhoods built around detention lakes and Oyster Creek stay humid and shaded, which favors algae on north walls, fences and pool decks.

## Cypress

### Facts
- Cypress is an unincorporated community in Harris County, entirely within the City of Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction. It lies along US 290, about 20 miles northwest of Houston. It is not a city, so it has no city code or city stormwater ordinance. Harris County regulations and local MUDs apply. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress,_Texas ; https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/cypress-tx-harris-county)
- The Texas State Historical Association (TSHA) describes Cypress as more a region than a single town, covering over 100 square miles in the Cypress Creek and Little Cypress Creek watersheds. Settlers arrived by 1831, and German immigrants settled along Cypress Creek in the 1840s. (Source: https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/cypress-tx-harris-county)
- Population of the Cypress CDP was 184,851 in 2020 (confirm). (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress,_Texas)
- Stormwater: the Regulations of Harris County for Stormwater Quality Management apply in all unincorporated areas. They prohibit discharging anything into the MS4 that is not entirely stormwater, apart from listed exceptions. Harris County Engineering administers them. (Source: https://resources.nctcog.org/envir/SEEclean/stormwater/BMP/docs/Harris%20County_Stormwater%20Quality_regs_080916.pdf ; https://www.cleanwaterways.org/Resources/Professionals)
- Water: most Cypress homes get water from MUDs or water districts. Many belong to the North Harris County Regional Water Authority (NHCRWA), which carries out the Harris-Galveston Subsidence District plan to move from groundwater to surface water: 30% by 2010, 60% by 2025, 80% by 2035. (Source: https://www.nhcrwa.com/ ; https://www.wcid132.org/)
- Example: Harris County WCID 132 serves parts of Cypresswood and Cypressdale, buys surface water from NHCRWA, and operates under an annual Subsidence District permit. (Source: https://www.wcid132.org/)
- Because watering and drought rules are set by each MUD's drought plan, not by one city, residents have to check their own district. (Inference from the MUD structure above. No single Cypress-wide rule exists.)
- The Cypress Creek watershed covers over 300 square miles of northwest Harris County. It got nearly 30 inches of rain in Hurricane Harvey (2017), which caused record flooding along the creek. More than 13,200 homes in the watershed have flooded since the Flood Control District's buyout program began in 1985. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/cy-fair/environment/three-years-after-harvey-harris-county-home-buyouts-lag-in-response-to-cypress-creek-flooding/ ; https://sspeed.rice.edu/harvey-related-projects-cypress)
- Bridgeland is an 11,400-acre master-planned community developed by Howard Hughes since 2006. It plans about 900 acres of lakes, and its lakes absorbed Cypress Creek overflow during Harvey. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeland_Community,_Texas ; https://developingresilience.uli.org/case/bridgeland/)
- Towne Lake, developed by Caldwell Companies since 2006, centers on a 300-acre recreational lake and is planned for about 2,400 acres. (Source: https://caldwellcos.com/development/communities/towne-lake)
- HOA example: a Villages of Cypress Lakes HOA newsletter says roofs, driveways and sidewalks shall be clean and free of mildew, and that gutters fill with pine needles and leaves and must be cleaned regularly. (confirm exact wording and that this is the association's own text) (Source: https://myneighborhoodnews.com/february-news-from-your-hoa-6171 ; https://www.crest-management.com/Communities/Villages-of-Cypress-Lakes-Community-Association/FAQ)

### Angles for a unique page
1. **No city, so the HOA and the MUD set the rules.** In Cypress, exterior cleanliness rules come from deed restrictions such as Bridgeland, Towne Lake and Cypress Lakes. Water-use limits come from the MUD's drought plan, and runoff falls under Harris County's stormwater regulations. Explain who to check with.
2. **Lake and creek communities.** Bridgeland, Towne Lake and homes along Cypress Creek sit next to water that collects runoff. Rinse-water handling and gentle cleaners near shorelines, docks and bulkheads are relevant topics.
3. **Mildew on roofs and gutters.** Local HOA language names roofs, driveways and sidewalks staying mildew-free, and gutters clogged with pine needles. This supports soft washing roofs and cleaning gutters.
4. **Newer homes, newer materials.** Much of Cypress is post-2000 construction with fiber-cement siding, stone accents, pavers and stained wood fences. Soft washing protects these materials. (Housing era not verified with a citable source; keep it general.)
5. **Harvey history along Cypress Creek.** Older neighborhoods near the creek may still show flood lines on fences and brick. Mention as context only.
