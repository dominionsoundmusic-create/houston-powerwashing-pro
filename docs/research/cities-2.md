## Pearland

### Facts

- Pearland is a city mainly in Brazoria County, with parts in Fort Bend and Harris Counties. 2020 census population: 125,828. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearland,_Texas)
- The town began as a railroad siding on the Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe Railway in 1882. The post office opened in 1893 as "Mark Belt" and was renamed Pearland that November because of the many pear trees there. The 1900 Galveston hurricane destroyed the fruit trees, and the 1918 freeze ended the orange and fig revival. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearland,_Texas ; https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/pearland-tx)
- Growth came fast and recently. Population went from 18,697 (1990) to 37,640 (2000), then grew 142% from 2000 to 2010. In the 1990s, developers bought large tracts and filled former rice fields with houses. So most of the housing stock dates from the 1990s onward. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearland,_Texas)
- Master-planned communities named in the city article include Sunrise Lakes, Southern Trails, Southdown, Lakes of Highland Glen and Shadow Creek Ranch. Silverlake and Country Place are in unincorporated areas near Pearland, not inside the city. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearland,_Texas)
- Shadow Creek Ranch: Pearland annexed the land in 1996 to 1998, home sales began in 2001, and the first phase dug four lakes. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Creek_Ranch)
- Shadow Creek Ranch rules: the HOA's community newsletter told residents that mildew and lack of home maintenance are against the deed restrictions. Its recorded Architectural Review Committee guidelines require any paint on stucco to contain a mildew retardant, and painting a fence or house needs an ARC application first. Owners must maintain the driveways serving their lots. (Source: https://662126957de34dbcca7a-fdbcf82d3dfc08785157cf0d6fc8ed50.ssl.cf1.rackcdn.com/1106SCR.pdf ; https://shadowcreekranchhoa.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/SEARCHABLE-Architectural-Review-Committee-Guidelines-recorded-4-2021.pdf ; https://shadowcreekranchhoa.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/SCR-BISCAYNE-Declaration-Searchable-6.8.2021.pdf)
- Silverlake HOA (Crest Management) does one deed-restriction inspection a month. Violation letters give a deadline to fix the problem, and there is no further action if it is fixed in time. (Source: https://www.crest-management.com/Communities/Silverlake-Homeowners-Association/FAQ)
- Water: Pearland uses both groundwater and surface water. The city's 10 wells draw from the Evangeline and Chicot aquifers. Surface water comes through agreements with the City of Houston and the Gulf Coast Water Authority. (Source: https://www.pearlandtx.gov/departments/pearland-water/water-production-185)
- Pearland's $175 million Surface Water Treatment Plant (south of Magnolia Parkway) began pumping in late 2024. It treats about 10 million gallons a day drawn from the Gulf Coast Water Authority's American Canal (Brazos River basin), and there are plans to expand it to 20 MGD. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/pearland-friendswood/government/2024/10/31/pearlands-new-surface-water-treatment-plant-begins-pumping-water/ ; https://www.pearlandtx.gov/departments/engineering-and-public-works/projects-division/project-updates/surface-water-treatment-plant)
- Stormwater: Pearland's Code of Ordinances Chapter 30 has an Article VI, "Illicit Discharge Prohibitions" (Sec. 30-143, illicit connections and discharge, and Sec. 30-144, specific prohibitions). (Source: https://library.municode.com/tx/pearland/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH30UT)
- Pearland's drought plan: Stage 1 asks for voluntary watering limits of twice a week on your trash day and no watering from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. Stage 2 makes those limits mandatory. Runoff that streams 50 feet or more down the street, or pools in the street more than a quarter inch deep, is discouraged. Unlike Conroe and Tomball, no Pearland source found here says outright that washing hard surfaces is banned in Stage 2. (Source: https://www.pearlandtx.gov/Home/Components/News/News/4662/231 ; https://www.pearland.com/msg/city-pearland-maintains-stage-two.php?p=1676027)
- Hurricane Harvey: more than 1,700 homes and over 50 businesses in Pearland took on structural flooding, mostly along Clear Creek. Clear Creek is on the state 303(d) list of waters impaired for bacteria. (Source: https://www.pearlandtx.gov/Home/Components/News/News/3145/)
- During Harvey, the Mary's Creek gauge in Pearland recorded 49.2 inches of rain, a state record for a tropical system at the time. (Source: https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/pearland/news/article/Hurricane-Harvey-brings-record-rains-to-Pearland-12169450.php)
- Texas HOA law (applies in all four cities): before an HOA can fine an owner, it must send written notice by certified mail giving a reasonable time to fix a curable violation. If the owner fixes it within that time, no fine can be charged. A 2025 change requires the notice within 180 days of the violation. (Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-11/chapter-209/section-209-006/)

### Old-site claims checked
- "Groundwater across Brazoria County carries dissolved iron, so sprinklers leave rust stains": **unverified**. No public source found ties Pearland city water to iron staining. City water is now a mix of wells and treated surface water. Rust fans from sprinklers are a common general problem, especially on private irrigation wells, but the site should not claim Pearland water is iron-heavy.
- "Gulf Coast prairie clay that swells and shrinks": **not verified in this pass**. Leave it out unless sourced to NRCS soil survey data.

### Angles for a unique page
1. HOA paperwork is the main driver: Shadow Creek Ranch counts mildew as a deed violation and wants mildew-retardant paint on stucco, and Silverlake inspects monthly. Texas law gives a cure window before any fine, so a timely wash can close a violation letter.
2. These are 1990s to 2020s homes on former rice fields: brick and stucco fronts, wide concrete driveways, and lake-lot backyards in Shadow Creek Ranch. That calls for a soft-wash approach on stucco and paint.
3. Clear Creek flood history and the impaired-for-bacteria listing: post-storm silt and mildew cleanup, and why wash water should not go down the storm drain (Chapter 30 illicit discharge rules).
4. Water timing: the trash-day watering schedule and runoff limits during drought stages, so ask about water use before booking in a dry summer.
5. Rust from sprinklers as a general question, worded carefully with no claim about Pearland's water quality.

## Spring

### Facts

- Spring is a census-designated place (CDP), not a city. It has no city government and lies mostly in unincorporated northwest Harris County, with part in unincorporated Montgomery County, inside Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction. 2020 census population: 62,559. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring,_Texas ; https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/springcdptexas/PST045224)
- Because it is unincorporated, Harris County's own stormwater rules apply. The "Regulations of Harris County, Texas for Stormwater Quality Management" cover all unincorporated areas. They allow routine pavement washing only when it is done without detergents, other chemicals or heat and with no other pollutants. Wash water with cleaning chemicals falls outside that exception. (Source: https://resources.nctcog.org/envir/SEEclean/stormwater/BMP/docs/Harris%20County_Stormwater%20Quality_regs_080916.pdf)
- Harris County Flood Control District runs the county's stormwater permit compliance program. (Source: https://www.hcfcd.org/Activity/Additional-Programs/Stormwater-Quality/Stormwater-Permit-Compliance)
- Water usually comes from a MUD (municipal utility district), not a city. Most Spring neighborhoods inside the North Harris County Regional Water Authority are on that plan. NHCRWA must move off groundwater to meet Harris-Galveston Subsidence District rules: 30% reduction by 2010, 60% by 2025 and 80% by 2035. 143 MUDs joined its groundwater reduction plan. (Source: https://www.nhcrwa.com/ ; https://www.nhcrwa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/GRP-2003.pdf)
- History: settled by Germans around 1840. The railroad arrived in 1871, and Spring became a railroad town in the early 1900s. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring,_Texas)
- Old Town Spring, the original town at Spring-Cypress and Hardy roads, covers about 1 square kilometer. It is a restored early-1900s railroad village with about 150 shops in older buildings and cottages. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring,_Texas)
- The Wunsche Bros. building on Midway Street dates from 1902 and is a Texas Historical Commission recorded landmark. (Source: https://www.homes.com/local-guide/spring-tx/old-town-spring-neighborhood/) *Secondary source; confirm on the THC atlas before using.*
- Trees: the Spring Creek Greenway runs through dense stands of loblolly pine, oak, sweetgum and bottomland hardwood. Neighborhoods such as Spring Creek Forest are known for tall loblolly pines on wooded lots. (Source: https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/texas/spring-creek-greenway-trail ; https://byjoandco.com/2022/02/25/spring-creek-forest-spring-tx-neighborhood/) *Secondary sources; fine for general color, not for hard numbers.*
- Pine and oak pollen: Houston-area tree pollen peaks February to April, and oak peaks in March. The Houston Health Department often reports "very heavy" tree pollen then, and the pollen leaves a visible yellow coat on cars, sidewalks and outdoor surfaces. (Source: https://www.chron.com/news/houston-texas/article/tree-pollen-count-allergy-season-20197821.php ; https://www.houstonhealth.org/news/newsletter/houston-healthscope/houston-healthscope-march-27-2026)
- Cypress Creek flooding: more than 8,700 homes flooded along Cypress Creek in Harvey (2017), and about 1,700 in the 2016 Tax Day flood. Spring and Klein neighborhoods hit hard include Ponderosa Forest, Westador, Champion Forest and Wimbledon Champions. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/spring-klein/environment/2020/12/21/flooding-to-remain-reality-for-many-spring-klein-homes-along-cypress-creek-experts-say/)
- Gleannloch Farms is a master-planned community off Champion Forest Drive between Spring-Cypress and the Grand Parkway (77379). Its community association publishes design guidelines for each section and enforces deed restrictions. (Source: https://gleannlochfarmsca.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Design-Guidelines-Section-34.pdf)
- Texas HOA notice-and-cure rule (Property Code 209.006) applies here too; see the Pearland section. (Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-11/chapter-209/section-209-006/)

### Old-site claims checked
- The pine canopy and wooded-suburb description is broadly supported (sources above).
- "Roof valleys streak first because pine needles hold water": a reasonable general explanation, but no public source specific to Spring. Present it as general roof-cleaning knowledge, not a local statistic.

### Angles for a unique page
1. No city hall: Harris County's stormwater rules govern runoff (pavement washing only without detergents, chemicals or heat), and your MUD runs your water. That is a real difference from incorporated cities.
2. Pine canopy: needles in valleys and gutters, the February to April yellow pollen coat, and shaded north walls and driveways that stay green. Roof soft washing and gutter-line staining belong together here.
3. Cypress Creek flood history in Champion Forest, Ponderosa Forest and similar areas: cleaning after high water, and silt lines on brick and fences.
4. Old Town Spring's older buildings and cottages need gentle, low-pressure work on aged wood and mortar, and timing around shop hours for commercial properties.
5. Strong HOAs in master-planned areas (Gleannloch Farms and others) with section-by-section design guidelines.

## Conroe

### Facts

- Conroe is the county seat of Montgomery County. 2020 census population: 89,956. The Census Bureau named it the fastest-growing large US city from July 2015 to July 2016. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conroe,_Texas)
- It grew out of lumber. Isaac Conroe, a Houston lumberman, founded a sawmill here in 1881, and the town ("Conroe's Switch") grew around timber cut from the piney woods. (Source: https://www.visitconroe.com/plan/history/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conroe,_Texas)
- Sam Houston National Forest covers about 163,000 acres of pine and hardwood 50 miles north of Houston, between Huntsville, Conroe, Cleveland and Richards. About 47,609 of those acres are in Montgomery County. (Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/texas/offices/sam-houston-national-forest ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Houston_National_Forest)
- Lake Conroe was built by the San Jacinto River Authority (SJRA) with the City of Houston as a water-supply reservoir. The dam was finished in January 1973. Houston owns two-thirds, SJRA one-third. Normal pool is 201 feet above sea level. (Source: https://www.sjra.net/lakeconroe/history/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Conroe)
- SJRA's Lake Conroe rules say no one may build, operate or maintain a pier, dock, floating boathouse, wharf or other structure on the reservoir or Authority land without an SJRA permit. A waterfront owner has no automatic right to these structures, and SJRA charges an annual fee per structure. SJRA's jurisdiction reaches the 201-foot contour. (Source: https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf ; https://www.sjra.net/lakeconroe/licensing-permitting/licensing-my-residential-structure/)
- The SJRA Lake Conroe Watershed Protection Plan names stormwater runoff from development around the lake as a main pollution source. Nutrients (phosphorus, nitrogen) and bacteria are the listed concerns. (Source: https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Lake-Conroe-Watershed-Protection-Plan.pdf)
- Harvey: Lake Conroe reached 206.2 feet (old record 205.60 in 1994). The dam released a record 79,100 cubic feet per second, more than twice the 1994 record. (Source: https://www.chron.com/news/houston-weather/hurricaneharvey/article/harvey-lake-conroe-dam-record-release-texas-12044717.php)
- Water: SJRA runs a 30 million gallon per day surface water plant at the Lake Conroe dam that treats lake water and pipes it 55 miles to partner utilities. Montgomery County's groundwater reduction plan contracts began in 2015 after the Lone Star Groundwater Conservation District limited pumping. In August 2025, Conroe and SJRA settled their long dispute over the plan. (Source: https://www.sjra.net/2022/10/turning-lake-conroe-water-into-drinking-water/ ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/conroe-montgomery/government/2025/08/15/san-jacinto-river-authority-settles-lawsuit-with-conroe-over-groundwater-plan/)
- Stormwater: TCEQ classifies Conroe as a Phase II (small) MS4 city, so it must run best practices to keep pollutants out of its storm drains. (Source: https://www.cityofconroe.org/services/engineering/site_development/swppp.php)
- Irrigation rules: from May 1 to October 31, Conroe limits sprinkler systems to 6 p.m. to 10 a.m. Odd addresses water Tuesday and Friday; even addresses Wednesday and Saturday. Repeat violations carry escalating fines. (Source: https://cityofconroe.org/Documents/Services/Utility%20Billing/Utility%20Billing%20Inserts/2025/Watering%20Regulations%20Brochure.pdf ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/conroe-montgomery/government/2024/06/20/conroe-city-council-passes-irrigation-restrictions-from-may-october/)
- Drought: under Conroe's Stage 2 drought rules (mandatory), using water to wash sidewalks, driveways and buildings is prohibited. Stage 1 is voluntary. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/conroe-montgomery/government/2023/09/08/conroe-under-stage-1-drought-with-portion-of-city-under-stage-2/ ; https://www.cityofconroe.org/services/water_conservation/index.php)
- Grand Central Park (Conroe) has an HOA. Its recorded declaration sets deed restrictions on exterior paint, landscaping, fencing and parking, and exterior changes need advance approval. (Source: https://www.fsrtx.com/connect_cws/docs/wd138_Deed_Restrictions.pdf ; https://tx-hoa.net/txhoa_list.php?mastertable=txtitle1&masterkey1=802184150) *Exterior-maintenance wording not read in full; check before quoting.*

### Old-site claims checked
- Docks, boathouses and bulkheads on Lake Conroe are real and SJRA-permitted (supported). The worry that runoff reaches the lake is supported in general by the watershed protection plan.
- "Conroe lots run larger and driveways longer": **unverified** as a general statement; leave it out or soften it.

### Angles for a unique page
1. Lakefront property: SJRA-permitted docks, boathouses and bulkheads, cleaning right over a drinking-water reservoir, and a watershed plan that flags nutrients and runoff. Ask what is sprayed and where the rinse goes.
2. Conroe drought Stage 2 explicitly bans washing sidewalks, driveways and buildings, and summer irrigation runs on an odd/even schedule. Booking timing really matters here.
3. Piney woods next to Sam Houston National Forest: pine needles, pollen and shade-driven mildew on roofs, decks and fences.
4. Harvey's record lake level and dam release: flood residue on lakefront and West Fork properties.
5. Fast growth: a mix of newer HOA communities (Grand Central Park) and older in-town and lake homes, so a different approach for each surface.

## Tomball

### Facts

- Tomball is an incorporated city in northwest Harris County. 2020 census population: 12,341. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomball,_Texas ; https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/tomballcitytexas)
- Tomball was platted in 1907 on the Trinity and Brazos Valley Railway. On December 2, 1907, Peck was renamed Tomball after Thomas Ball, the former congressman and railroad lawyer credited with routing the line through town. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/113/History-of-Tomball ; https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/tomball-tx)
- The city's history page says Tomball is the highest point in Harris County. Heavy freight loaded here could coast downhill toward Galveston. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/113/History-of-Tomball)
- The 1907 depot is a four-room building on the north side of Main Street. It is now the Tomball Depot Museum at the Railroad Depot Plaza, 201 S. Elm St. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/9197/Tomball-Depot-Museum-Historical-Overview-PDF)
- German heritage: the city's German Heritage Festival celebrates German families who settled around 1840. The historic downtown has antique shops, and the Tomball Museum Center has about a dozen restored historic buildings. (Source: https://www.tomballtx.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=5864 ; https://tomballtx.gov/110/Attractions)
- Water: Tomball runs its own water system on groundwater from the Evangeline and Chicot aquifers. It sits inside the North Harris County Regional Water Authority, which is moving the area to surface water under Harris-Galveston Subsidence District rules. A planned project of about $119.6 million would bring surface water to several Tomball plants. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/Archive/ViewFile/Item/2574 ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/tomball-magnolia/government/2025/03/05/multimillion-dollar-projects-target-water-infrastructure/)
- Stormwater: under the Clean Water Act and Texas Water Code, Tomball holds a stormwater permit and runs a Stormwater Management Program to cut pollutants in runoff. It promotes "Only Rain Down the Storm Drain." The Stormwater Management Manual is in Code of Ordinances Sec. 18-259. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/533/Stormwater-Management-Program)
- Drought stages are set by demand as a share of production: Stage 1 at 60%, Stage 2 at 70%, Stage 3 at 80%, Stage 4 at 90%, Stage 5 at 95%, each for three days in a row. Watering is allowed 7 p.m. to 5 a.m. only: even addresses Sunday and Thursday, odd addresses Saturday and Wednesday. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/709/Drought-Status ; https://tomballtx.gov/FAQ.aspx?QID=154)
- Under Tomball's Stage 2 (mandatory) drought rules, washing sidewalks, driveways, parking lots and other hard surfaces is banned, as are washing buildings (except for fire protection) and flushing gutters. Stage 1 asks residents to voluntarily limit power washing. Tomball was the first Southeast Texas city to impose mandatory restrictions in the 2023 drought. (Source: https://www.houstonchronicle.com/neighborhood/tomball-magnolia/article/tomball-drought-stage-2-water-restrictions-18286062.php ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/tomball-magnolia/government/2023/07/19/tomball-enters-stage-1-drought-voluntary-conservation-measures-recommended/ ; https://abc13.com/tomball-water-restrictions-conservation-houston-deer-park-voluntary/13622937/)
- Flooding: the north side of Tomball drains to the Spring Creek watershed and the south side to Willow Creek. Harvey dropped about 30 inches on Tomball and Magnolia over four days. Willow Creek topped its 100-year level by about a foot, and homes near Spring, Willow, Mill and Walnut creeks flooded. This came a year after the April and May 2016 floods. (Source: https://tomballtx.gov/207/Floodplain-Management ; https://communityimpact.com/tomball-magnolia/economic-development/2017/10/04/tomball-magnolia-weather-the-storm-begin-recovery/)
- NWS flood gauges cover Spring Creek at Tomball and Willow Creek near Tomball. NWS lists the Foxwood subdivision as one area where major flooding begins. (Source: https://water.noaa.gov/gauges/TMBT2 ; https://water.noaa.gov/gauges/lwct2)
- Neighborhoods: Wildwood at Northpointe and Villages of Northpointe are established master-planned areas. Rosehill Reserve (west of town on FM 2920) is a newer 360-acre community of brick and stone homes. (Source: https://www.har.com/wildwood-at-northpointe/masterplan_100 ; https://byjoandco.com/2021/11/27/best-master-planned-communities-neighborhoods-in-tomball-tx/) *Secondary/real-estate sources; use for names only.*

### Old-site claims checked
- "Well water causes rust stains on Tomball properties": **partly unverified**. City water is groundwater, and many acreage homes outside town are on private wells. Claims of high iron appear only on water-softener sales pages, not public sources. Wording like "if your irrigation runs on a private well, orange fan-shaped stains can come from iron in the water" is safe. "Tomball water is high in iron" is not.
- "Pine pollen and sap, shaded mildew": general Houston-area pollen timing is sourced (see Spring). No Tomball-specific canopy source found.

### Angles for a unique page
1. Drought rules hit pressure washing directly: Stage 2 bans washing driveways, sidewalks and buildings and even flushing gutters, and Tomball was first in the region to go mandatory in 2023. Check the city's drought status before booking.
2. A small railroad town with a historic downtown (1907 depot, restored museum buildings, antique shops), so older masonry and painted wood need gentle cleaning.
3. Groundwater town with private wells on surrounding acreage: rust from irrigation as a "check your well" question, plus long fence runs and outbuildings.
4. Two watersheds (Spring Creek north, Willow Creek south) and back-to-back 2016 and 2017 floods: post-flood silt and mildew on fences and siding.
5. Newer master-planned areas (Northpointe, Rosehill Reserve) with HOA standards, next to older in-town homes.
