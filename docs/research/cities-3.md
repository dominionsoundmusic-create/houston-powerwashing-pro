## Kingwood (City of Houston, Harris County, small part in Montgomery County)

### Facts
- Kingwood was annexed by the City of Houston, effective December 11, 1996. The annexation added about 43,000 people to the city. The area still uses "Kingwood, TX" mailing addresses. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston ; https://www.kingwood.com/community/annexation.php)
- Kingwood is a master-planned community of about 14,000 acres, founded in 1970 by King Ranch and Friendswood Development Company, an Exxon subsidiary (the name joins the two). The first village opened in 1971. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston ; https://m.chron.com/discoverhouston/suburbs/article/Kingwood-Northeast-Houston-boasts-a-Livable-5752557.php)
- "The Livable Forest" was a slogan Friendswood Development Company created for Kingwood, referring to its many trees. Kingwood remains heavily wooded. (Source: https://www.kingwood.com/community/livable_forest.php ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston)
- Kingwood has a private greenbelt trail system of 75+ miles (Wikipedia gives about 85 miles of wooded trails) **(sources differ on the mileage; say "more than 75 miles")**. (Source: https://www.kingwood.com/community/greenbelt.php ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston)
- The Kingwood Service Association (KSA), a nonprofit, maintains community-wide assets such as greenbelts, parks (for example River Grove, Deer Ridge, Creekwood Nature Area, North Park Recreation Area, East End Park) and recreational facilities. After annexation, upkeep of most public spaces stayed with community associations, trails associations and KSA rather than the city. (Source: https://www.kingwood.com/community/kingwood_service_association.php ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston)
- Kingwood is divided into named villages, each with its own community association that enforces deed restrictions. **(Count conflicts: sources say 21 or 25 villages; avoid a number.)** (Source: https://www.houstonproperties.com/houston-neighborhoods/kingwood-master-planned ; https://www.texasalmanac.com/places/kingwood)
- Village deed restrictions require prior written approval from the Architectural Control Committee for changes such as exterior siding, paint and roof replacement, submitted through the association's management company before work starts. (Source: http://www.kingwoodassociationmanagement.com/ ; http://kingwoodassociationmanagement.com/kingwoodmgt/document_view.asp?id=15)
- At least one Kingwood village association (Kingwood Place West, Crest Management) states that deed restriction inspections are typically monthly, with violation letters giving a deadline to correct. (Source: https://www.crest-management.com/Communities/Kingwood-Place-West-Community-Association/FAQ)
- Because Houston has no zoning, the City of Houston Legal Department runs a deed restriction enforcement team; residents can file complaints (hotline 832-393-6333), and the city can send violation letters and go to court. This applies to Kingwood as part of the city. (Source: https://houstontx.gov/legal/dr-complaint.html ; https://www.houstontx.gov/council/committees/qol/20240815/deed-restrictions.pdf)
- City of Houston Code Chapter 47, Article XII (Storm Water Discharges) makes it an offense to introduce into the MS4 "any discharge that is not composed entirely of storm water" (Sec. 47-741(a)). (Source: https://library.municode.com/tx/houston/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH47WASE_ARTXIISTWADI_DIV1INGE_S47-601DE)
- During Hurricane Harvey (2017), Lake Houston and the West Fork of the San Jacinto River overflowed into thousands of Kingwood homes and businesses; reporting cites more than 4,400 structures flooded in Humble and Kingwood along the West Fork, and hundreds of rescues in Kingwood. (Source: https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/kingwood/news/article/Three-years-after-Harvey-Kingwood-continues-to-15524207.php ; https://patch.com/texas/humble-kingwood/hurricane-harvey-rising-san-jacinto-river-impacting-kingwood-humble-area-homes)
- Lake Houston, created by damming the San Jacinto River, is one of the City of Houston's main drinking-water sources. (Source: https://www.houstonpublicworks.org/sites/g/files/nwywnm456/files/doc/003-water-quality-report-2022.pdf)
- Kingwood water supply detail **(sources conflict / unverified)**: some sources say Kingwood (Houston Utility District 5) runs on groundwater wells in the Evangeline and Chicot aquifers, others say most water comes from Lake Houston. Do not state either on the page. (Source: https://www.ewg.org/tapwater/system.php?pws=TX1010348)
- Housing age: the median year built is about 1990; roughly 19.5% of homes were built in the 1970s and 28.4% in the 1980s (aggregator data, not official). (Source: https://www.point2homes.com/US/Neighborhood/TX/Kingwood-Demographics.html)
- Most of Kingwood is in Harris County (Humble ISD); a small Montgomery County portion is served by New Caney ISD. (Source: https://www.har.com/blog_109232_77339---zip-code-of-kingwood-texas ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingwood,_Houston)

### Angles for a unique page
1. Shade and tree canopy: under the "Livable Forest" pines and hardwoods, roofs, north walls, fences and driveways stay damp, so mildew and algae return faster than in open subdivisions. Explain why soft washing (not high pressure) suits shaded roofs and siding.
2. Village associations plus City of Houston deed-restriction enforcement: two layers of oversight. A good company should know whether a village ACC cares about appearance-related upkeep, and should never alter colors or surfaces in a way that needs ACC approval.
3. City of Houston storm-drain rule (Chapter 47): wash water must not go down street inlets that feed the San Jacinto River and Lake Houston, which is a drinking-water source. Ask how a company handles runoff.
4. 1970s and 1980s housing stock: older concrete, aging wood fences and older roofs need gentler, more careful cleaning.
5. Post-Harvey homes along the West Fork: flood-line staining and silt on lower walls, driveways and fences.

## League City (Galveston County; small part in Harris County)

### Facts
- League City had 114,392 residents at the 2020 census. It is in Galveston County, in the Greater Houston and Galveston Bay area on the south side of Clear Lake, with a small residential/commercial area north of Clear Creek in Harris County. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_City,_Texas ; https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/leaguecitycitytexas/PST045224)
- Clear Creek forms the city's northern edge and flows into Clear Lake and on to Galveston Bay; the city drains to the Clear Creek and Dickinson Bayou watersheds. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_City,_Texas ; https://www.galvnews.com/news/free/article_ac7314c7-00d4-5e6a-8d44-924da72c15d7.html)
- Hurricane Harvey flooded about 7,700 League City homes, about 23% of residences, with 1,450 having major damage (at least 18 inches of water inside). Clear Creek ran almost 16 feet at I-45 against a flood stage of about 6 feet. (Source: https://www.galvnews.com/news/free/article_ac7314c7-00d4-5e6a-8d44-924da72c15d7.html)
- After FEMA updated coastal flood maps following Katrina (2005) and Ike (2008), 3,730 League City homes were listed in the 100-year floodplain and 7,378 in the 500-year floodplain (2019 reporting). (Source: https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/bayarea/news/article/Why-thousands-more-League-City-homes-are-listed-14363093.php)
- The town is named for J.C. League, a Galveston businessman; it was renamed League City in 1902. (Source: https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/4276/Who-Was-JC-League)
- In 1907 League arranged two railcars of live oaks, planted along the main roads with G.W. Butler and his son Milby. These "Butler Oaks," now over 100 years old, still line Main Street and nearby streets in the Historic District (500 to 900 blocks of East Main Street / FM 518). (Source: https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/4276/Who-Was-JC-League ; https://visitleaguecity.visitwidget.com/tours/explore-league-city-s-historic-district)
- In the Historic Overlay District, a Certificate of Appropriateness is required for exterior construction, alteration, restoration or demolition visible from a public right-of-way; the city's FAQ says to contact the Historic Preservation Officer before painting masonry. (Source: https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/45965/Historic-District-Frequently-Asked-Questions ; https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/4685/Historic-Overlay-District)
- League City's Code Chapter 43 (Clean Water) covers prohibited discharges to the municipal separate storm sewer system (MS4). (Source: https://library.municode.com/TX/League_City/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PAIICOOR_CH43CLWA)
- The city's Stormwater Pollution page describes public reporting of illicit discharges and storm-drain marking to discourage dumping. (Source: https://leaguecitytx.gov/1286/Stormwater-Pollution)
- Drinking water comes from the City of Houston's Southeast Water Purification Plant (Trinity River water) and the Gulf Coast Water Authority's Thomas Mackey plant in Texas City (Brazos River water). (Source: https://www.leaguecitytx.gov/3310/League-Citys-Water-Sources)
- The Drought Contingency Plan's Stage 1 asks customers to voluntarily avoid outdoor watering between 9 a.m. and 7 p.m. and to water twice a week on days matching their trash days; later stages can make restrictions mandatory. (Source: https://leaguecitytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4782/Drought-Contingency-Plan-PDF?bidId= ; https://library.municode.com/tx/league_city/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PAIICOOR_CH114UT_ARTVIDRCOPL_S114-230EN)
- League City is within the Harris-Galveston Subsidence District, created by the Legislature in 1975 to regulate groundwater pumping in Harris and Galveston counties; the city received an HGSD water conservation grant in 2020. (Source: https://hgsubsidence.org/about/ ; https://hgsubsidence.org/2021/11/01/water-conservation-grant-project-the-city-of-league-city/)
- Large HOA communities include South Shore Harbour, Victory Lakes and Tuscan Lakes. (Source: https://www.creekstonere.com/neighborhood-guides/south-shore-harbour/ ; https://hoa-resource.com/victory-lakes-homeowners-association-league-city-tx/)
- **(unverified, do not use as fact)**: whether the NWS Houston/Galveston forecast office counts as "in League City" (its listed address is 1353 FM 646, Dickinson). (Source: https://www.weather.gov/hgx/)

### Angles for a unique page
1. Bay-side air: near Clear Lake and Galveston Bay, moisture and salt-laden air leave film on siding, windows, outdoor metal and pool decks; explain why gentle soft washing and thorough rinsing matter (keep it general; no corrosion statistics).
2. Historic District care: under the Butler Oaks, older homes and masonry; a good company should use low pressure on old brick and wood, and the owner should check with the city before anything beyond cleaning (painting masonry, alterations need a COA).
3. Flood and storm cleanup: Harvey flooded about 23% of homes; Clear Creek and Dickinson Bayou watersheds. Flood-line staining, silt on driveways and fences.
4. Storm drains go straight to Clear Creek, Clear Lake and Galveston Bay: ask how runoff and cleaning products are handled (Chapter 43).
5. Watering and drought stages: the city sets watering days and times in its drought plan; ask whether a job can be done during a declared stage.

## Friendswood (Galveston and Harris counties)

### Facts
- Friendswood had 41,213 residents at the 2020 census and lies in both Galveston and Harris counties. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendswood,_Texas ; https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/friendswoodcitytexas/PST045221)
- Friendswood was founded in 1895 as a Quaker colony led by T. Hadley Lewis and Frank J. Brown, who bought 1,538 acres at the headwaters of Clear Creek from J.C. League. It is described as the only permanent Texas town that began as a Quaker colony. (Source: https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=50156 ; https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/religious-society-of-friends)
- The early colony farmed fig orchards, Satsuma orange orchards and rice. (Source: https://www.ci.friendswood.tx.us/168/Community)
- Three creeks run through the city: Clear Creek, Cowart Creek and Mary's Creek; all three left their banks during Hurricane Harvey. (Source: https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/houston-texas/houston/article/Clear-Creek-flooding-reminds-Friendswood-15588102.php)
- During Harvey, early reports said about 3,000 Friendswood homes had flooded **(preliminary, mid-storm figure)**; the city issued 1,787 permits for Harvey repairs in 2017. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/pearland-friendswood/city-county/2017/08/27/hurricane-harvey-pearland-friendswood-emergency-personnel-monitor-weather-schools-close/ ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/pearland-friendswood/housing-real-estate/2020/07/20/friendswood-real-estate-flourishes-despite-harveys-toll-on-homes/)
- The city commissioned a Flood Vulnerability and Mitigation Study (Dr. Bedient and Associates, Rice University, 2019) and has pursued a Clear Creek flood-control project. (Source: https://www.ci.friendswood.tx.us/DocumentCenter/View/1292/04-2019-Friendswood-Flood-Vulnerability-and-Mitigation-Study---Dr-Bedient-and-Associates-Rice-University-PDF ; https://www.galvnews.com/news/friendswood-pushes-major-flood-control-upgrades-led-by-10m-clear-creek-project/article_19ae3b5b-932d-43f1-95f0-eb64904eddf4.html)
- Friendswood Code Chapter 86, Article V (Regulation of Stormwater Runoff) defines illicit connections to include conveyances that let non-stormwater discharges, including "wash water," into the storm drain system. (Source: https://library.municode.com/tx/friendswood/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICICO_CH86UT_ARTVRESTRU_DIV1GE)
- The city has a Stormwater Management Program (February 2025) under its MS4 permit, giving it legal authority to prohibit illicit discharges. (Source: https://ci.friendswood.tx.us/DocumentCenter/View/391/Storm-Water-Management-Plan-PDF)
- Friendswood buys surface water from the City of Houston's Southeast Water Purification Plant (Trinity River water) and is a co-participant in that plant; under 20% of its drinking water comes from groundwater. (Source: https://www.chron.com/neighborhood/article/Where-does-your-water-come-from-9798478.php ; https://www.houstonpublicworks.org/hpw-hosts-treatment-plant-tour-for-drinking-water-week)
- The city publishes an HOA contact list covering its many subdivision associations. (Source: https://ci.friendswood.tx.us/DocumentCenter/View/6144/HOA-CONTACT-LIST-2025)
- Wedgewood Village is one of the oldest subdivisions, with over 900 homes; its HOA enforces deed restrictions and requires plans for improvements under the Architectural Control section. (Source: https://www.wvhoa.net/home/about-us)
- The Forest of Friendswood is a deed-restricted community with large wooded lots and mature trees; its architectural review committee covers landscaping, fencing and property maintenance. (Source: https://www.houstonproperties.com/houston-neighborhoods/the-forest-of-friendswood)
- The city has tree and plant protection specifications for construction and a qualified tree list in its design criteria manual. (Source: https://www.ci.friendswood.tx.us/DocumentCenter/View/3648/01105---Tree-and-Plant-Protection-PDF)

### Angles for a unique page
1. Three creeks and Harvey: homes near Clear, Cowart and Mary's creeks deal with flood-line staining, silt and mildew after high water.
2. Wooded, older subdivisions (Wedgewood Village, Forest of Friendswood): shade, leaf litter and tannin stains on driveways and fences; HOA architectural rules cover fences and upkeep.
3. Two counties, one city: Galveston and Harris County homes share the same city stormwater code, where "wash water" is named as a non-stormwater discharge.
4. Quaker colony and small-town roots: older homes alongside newer ones, so a good company should adjust pressure to the material and age.
5. Water comes mostly from Houston's Southeast plant: worth noting the regional water system without telling people how to wash.

## Missouri City (mostly Fort Bend County, small part in Harris County)

### Facts
- Missouri City was incorporated on March 13, 1956, and had 74,259 residents at the 2020 census (67,358 in 2010). It is mostly in Fort Bend County with a small part in Harris County. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_City,_Texas ; https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/missouricitycitytexas)
- Quail Valley dates from 1969, was built around a country club and golf course, and has 15 subdivisions; home building mostly ended by the end of the 1970s. (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quail_Valley_(Missouri_City,_Texas))
- Sienna (formerly Sienna Plantation) is a roughly 10,000-acre master-planned community by Johnson Development near the Brazos River; first homes sold in 1996, and it dropped "Plantation" from its name in 2019. (Source: https://www.siennatx.com/about-sienna ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sienna,_Texas)
- Sienna's associations enforce community standards/deed restrictions (contact CommunityStandards@clubsienna.com), and their own maintenance updates describe cleaning mold and mildew off arbors, pillars, brick panels, walkways and monuments, soft washing, and cleaning and sealing wood fences in common areas. (Source: https://www.siennanet.com/deed-restrictions/ ; https://www.siennanet.com/landscape-and-maintenance/)
- The city's Code Enforcement "Common Code Concerns" page lists mildew, saying surfaces must be kept clean and sanitary, and requires fences and other accessory structures to be kept structurally sound and in good repair. Reported concerns are investigated within 2 business days. (Source: https://www.missouricitytx.gov/1194/Common-Code-Concerns ; https://www.missouricitytx.gov/236/Code-Enforcement)
- The city runs a Stormwater Hotline (281-403-8543) for reporting illegal dumping and illicit discharges, as part of its MS4 program. (Source: https://www.missouricitytx.gov/Faq.aspx?QID=250 ; https://missouricitytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/16473/PY4-COMC-MS4-Annual-Report-Final-20231227)
- The city's MS4 annual report records street sweeping of about 734 lane miles in one permit year. (Source: https://missouricitytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/16473/PY4-COMC-MS4-Annual-Report-Final-20231227)
- Under the 2025 Drought Contingency Plan, Stages 2 and 3 limit watering to 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. on assigned days; in Stages 3 and 4, water use for pavement or gutter washing is prohibited, and Stage 4 bans all non-essential use. (Source: https://missouricitytx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/17572/2025-FINAL-DCP ; https://communityimpact.com/houston/sugar-land-missouri-city/government/2025/04/02/missouri-city-updates-drought-contingency-plan-with-larger-affected-area/)
- The city's surface water treatment plant (built 2012, expanded 2020) treats Brazos River water bought from the Gulf Coast Water Authority. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/sugar-land-missouri-city/city-county/missouri-city-expands-surface-water-reuse-program-second-phase-aims-to-double-the-amount-of-water-treated/ ; https://www.missouricitytx.gov/980/Drought-Contingency-Plan)
- The Fort Bend Subsidence District, created by the Legislature in 1989, limits groundwater use, which pushes cities toward surface water. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/sugar-land-missouri-city/city-county/missouri-city-expands-surface-water-reuse-program-second-phase-aims-to-double-the-amount-of-water-treated/)
- During Harvey (2017), Fort Bend County ordered mandatory evacuations near the Brazos, including Sienna Plantation, and the Brazos set record heights; tornadoes on Aug. 26 damaged homes in Sienna and a shopping plaza on Texas Parkway. (Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/sugar-land-missouri-city/news/2017/08/27/mandatory-evacuations-ordered-brazos-river-areas/ ; https://communityimpact.com/top-stories/2017/09/09/surveying-the-destruction-left-in-harveys-aftermath/)

### Angles for a unique page
1. City code names mildew: Missouri City code enforcement lists mildew and fence condition as common concerns, so keeping siding, walks and fences clean is about code as well as looks.
2. Drought stages and pavement washing: in Stages 3 and 4 washing pavement is prohibited. A good company should check the current stage before washing a driveway or sidewalk.
3. Two eras of housing: 1970s Quail Valley golf-course homes (older brick, mature trees) and newer Sienna homes from 1996 on. Different surfaces, ages and HOA standards.
4. Brazos River lowlands: humidity, river-bottom soils and Harvey flood history mean mildew on fences and lower walls, and silt staining.
5. Report-a-discharge culture: the city runs a stormwater hotline; runoff from washing should not go into street drains.
