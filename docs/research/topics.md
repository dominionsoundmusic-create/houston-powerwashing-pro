## 1. Licensing, permits and sales tax

- Pressure washing is not one of the occupations licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Search extracts consistently say Texas has no state pressure washing license and that a general business registration is what applies. **Partly verified:** the TDLR program list (https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/programs.htm) could not be fetched to confirm directly. Secondary sources: (Source: https://hotsyhouston.com/do-i-need-a-contractors-license-for-pressure-washing/) (Source: https://gritwork.io/jobs/pressure-washing/licensing/) **(lower confidence: industry blog)**
- **City of Houston pressure washer permit (important):** the Houston Health Department issues a Pressure Washer Permit under its Special Waste program. To get the permit, "all equipment (booms, mats and recovery systems) must be tested by the City of Houston to ensure that all wastewater will be recovered and storm drains are protected." The application also asks for owner, manager and driver licenses and proof of financial responsibility for each vehicle and trailer, and requires the company name and phone number on both sides of the vehicle in 3-inch lettering. (Source: https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/11346/download, the 2026 Pressure Washer Permit Application) (Source: https://www.houstonhealth.org/services/permits/special-waste-permits/special-waste-service-companies)
- Houston Health Department guidance for special waste generators (restaurants and similar) says they "may only use a permitted pressure washing company that has a current and valid permit with the City of Houston Health Department, and who is equipped to collect all pressured wash water and prevent it from entering the environment." (Source: https://www.houstonhealth.org/media/271/download, the Waste Generator's FAQ) (Source: https://www.houstonconsumer.org/services/permits/special-waste-permits/special-waste-generators)
- **Open question (unverified):** it is not clear from the extracts whether the Health Department permit applies to every mobile washer working inside city limits or only to companies serving special waste generators (restaurants, grease areas, dumpster pads). Call the Houston Health Department Special Waste program to confirm before stating scope. Safe site wording: "In the City of Houston, companies that wash restaurant and commercial grease areas need a Houston Health Department pressure washer permit. Ask the company you are matched with whether they hold one."
- **Texas sales tax (Comptroller Rule 3.357):** per Comptroller publications, pressure washing buildings, sidewalks and parking lots is taxable as building or grounds cleaning (a nonresidential real property service). Labor to repair or remodel *residential* real property is not taxed. "Nonresidential real property" includes restaurants, offices and parking lots but excludes property used as a family dwelling (such as apartment complexes). (Source: https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-259.php) (Source: https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-111.php) (Source: https://star.comptroller.texas.gov/view/202203012L)
  - Practical meaning, **to verify** before publishing: commercial pressure washing in Texas is generally subject to sales tax, and residential exterior cleaning generally is not. Do not state this as tax advice. At most say "commercial cleaning in Texas may be subject to sales tax; your company can explain its invoice."

## 2. Wash water and stormwater

- **City of Houston Code of Ordinances, Ch. 47, Art. XII (Storm Water Discharges):** Sec. 47-741(a) says "A person commits an offense if the person threatens to introduce, introduces or causes to be introduced into the MS4 any discharge that is not composed entirely of storm water." MS4 means the city's municipal separate storm sewer system. (Source: https://library.municode.com/tx/houston/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=COOR_CH47WASE_ARTXIISTWADI_DIV1INGE_S47-601DE)
  - Exceptions in Sec. 47-741 (for example lawn watering or residential car washing) could not be read because the page fetch was blocked. **Unverified:** check the list of allowed non-stormwater discharges before saying anything about them.
- Houston Public Works Industrial Wastewater Service handles requests to discharge non-domestic wastewater (which can include washdown water) to the sanitary sewer: (832) 395-5800. Prohibited sewer discharges include anything that forms a floating layer of oil and grease or that can block sewers. (Source: https://www.houstonpublicworks.org/industrial-and-pretreatment-faqs)
- Houston residents report drainage and storm sewer problems through 311 (houstontx.gov/311 or the Houston 3-1-1 app). (Source: https://www.houstonpublicworks.org/stormwater-operations)
- **EPA:** EPA's NPDES best management practice fact sheet for vehicle maintenance and washing calls for containing wash water and sending it to the sanitary sewer (with permission) or collecting it for proper disposal, not letting it reach storm drains. (Source: https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-01/bmp-vehicle-maintenance-and-washing.pdf, EPA-832-F-21-032F, February 2023)
- EPA's "good housekeeping" guidance for businesses lists outdoor washing as a source of stormwater pollution. (Source: https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-11/documents/goodhousekeepingbusiness.pdf)
- **State agency guidance on mobile power washing (out of state, but describes the standard practice):** storm drains carry water to creeks and rivers without treatment. Wash water must not enter storm drain inlets. Acceptable containment includes waterproof tarps, plastic or rubber mats with berms (sand bags, water-filled tubes), plus vacuum recovery and discharge to a sanitary sewer only with the treatment plant's approval. (Source: https://www.michigan.gov/egle/-/media/Project/Websites/egle/Documents/Programs/WRD/Groundwater-Discharge/power-washing-wastewater.pdf, Michigan EGLE, rev. 11/2023) (Source: https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/epa.ohio.gov/Portals/41/sb/publications/powerwash.pdf, Ohio EPA)
- **TCEQ:** TCEQ administers the Texas Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (TPDES) stormwater permits. Its small-business guide "Basics of Stormwater Regulations" (RG-434) is the general reference. No TCEQ document specific to mobile pressure washing was found. (Source: https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/assistance/publications/rg-434.pdf/@@download/file/rg-434.pdf) **Unverified:** any TCEQ statement specific to residential pressure washing.
- **What a good company should do** (synthesized from the sources above, safe for site copy): block or cover nearby storm drain inlets; use berms, mats or a vacuum recovery system on commercial and grease jobs; never rinse grease, oil or cleaning solution into the street gutter; send collected water to an approved sanitary sewer connection or a licensed hauler; and on Houston commercial grease work, hold the Houston Health Department pressure washer permit.

## 4. Roof cleaning

- **Cause of black streaks:** the dark streaks on asphalt shingles are mainly algae. *Gloeocapsa magma* (a blue-green algae/cyanobacterium) is the most common species. The discoloration is brown to black and is often mistaken for soot, dirt, mold or tree droppings. (Source: https://www.asphaltroofing.org/algae-discoloration-of-roofs/) (Source: https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/blog/algae-resistance)
- **ARMA (Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association) technical bulletin:** algae discoloration can be lightened with a diluted chlorine bleach (sodium hypochlorite) solution, applied gently and rinsed gently with low-pressure water. Do not scrub, because friction loosens granules. ARMA says "high pressure washing systems are likely to damage asphalt roofing and should not be used." (Source: https://www.asphaltroofing.org/algae-moss-prevention-cleaning-asphalt-roofing-systems/) (Source: https://www.atlasroofing.com/img/Literature/ARMA_-_Algae_Discoloration_of_Roofs.pdf, ARMA technical bulletin revised January 2015). Do NOT publish the bleach ratio or dwell time.
- **GAF technical bulletin R-102 (Algae Staining on Shingled Roof Surfaces):** recommends a bleach-based wash rinsed with low pressure. GAF warns against power washing because it can dislodge granules and shorten shingle life. (Source: https://www.gaf.com/en-us/document-library/documents/technical-bulletins-&-notes/r-102-algae-staining-on-shingled-roof-surfaces.pdf)
- **Owens Corning:** dark streaks are caused by *Gloeocapsa magma*. Its algae-resistant shingles (StreakGuard) use copper-containing granules (from 3M) to inhibit growth. Its guidance: do not scrub or use high-pressure power washing, because that removes granules and shortens roof life. (Source: https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/blog/algae-resistance) (Source: https://www.owenscorning.com/en-us/roofing/streakguard)
- **CertainTeed:** algae-resistant (StreakFighter) shingles carry a limited algae-resistance warranty. The length depends on the product (extracts cite 10 to 30 years, with the longest terms requiring matching AR hip and ridge). (Source: https://www.certainteed.com/inspiration/how-tos/algae-resistant-shingles) (Source: https://www.certainteed.com/inspiration/projects/certainteeds-streakfighter-algae-resistant-technology). Exact terms are **unverified**: check the current warranty document.
- **Warranty implications:** the claim that "pressure washing voids your shingle warranty" appears widely, but only in contractor blogs and Wikipedia, not in manufacturer text found here. **Unverified.** Safe wording: "Shingle makers warn that high-pressure washing strips the protective granules, and damage from improper cleaning may not be covered. Check your roof warranty."
- **Zinc and copper strips:** ARMA says zinc strips near the ridge may reduce the regrowth of algae and moss, and zinc or copper strips can be considered when a roof is replaced. Rain washes metal ions down the roof, which inhibits growth. (Source: https://www.asphaltroofing.org/algae-moss-prevention-cleaning-asphalt-roofing-systems/)
- **Tile roofs:** Eagle Roofing (a concrete tile maker) has a page on cleaning concrete tile roofs. It favors water-only or very dilute cleaner compatible with concrete, warns that some products affect tile pH or etch the surface, and recommends using an experienced, insured roofing professional because walking on wet tile is dangerous and tiles can break. (Source: https://eagleroofing.com/2019/03/how-to-properly-clean-your-concrete-tile-roof/). Clay tile: low-pressure or soft wash is preferred to avoid cracks and chips. (Source: https://www.ecohome.net/en/guides/4076/the-definitive-guide-to-cleaning-treating-tile-roofs-concrete-clay-slate/) **(lower confidence)**
- **Metal roofs:** Metal Sales (a panel maker) advises checking the panel maker's cleaning recommendations and warranty first, since some warranties specify cleaners or methods. It recommends mild detergent and low pressure, avoiding abrasives that damage the coating. (Source: https://www.metalsales.us.com/blog/how-to-clean-and-maintain-a-metal-roof/)

## 5. Soft washing vs pressure washing; siding

- **Definitions (Angi):** soft washing uses low pressure plus a cleaning solution to kill and remove mold, algae and mildew, and is meant for delicate surfaces (painted surfaces, vinyl, wood, roofing, stucco, brick). Pressure washing uses high-pressure water and suits hard surfaces like concrete. (Source: https://www.angi.com/articles/soft-wash-better-regular-pressure-washing.htm)
- This Old House describes soft wash roof cleaning as using water pressure about that of a garden hose (no more than about 100 psi) together with a cleaning solution. (Source: https://www.thisoldhouse.com/roofing/roof-cleaning-cost)
- **James Hardie (fiber cement):** "Don't use a high-pressure power washer." Use a low-pressure garden hose and a soft to medium bristle (nonmetal) brush, work top to bottom, and wash the exterior every 6 to 12 months. (Source: https://www.jameshardie.com/blog/siding-durability/tips-for-cleaning-your-siding/) (Source: https://www.jameshardie.com/faq/)
- **Vinyl Siding Institute (VSI):** vinyl siding usually needs only mild soap and water from a garden hose. A power washer can be used with caution and the siding maker's instructions. Spray straight on at eye level and never aim upward, because that forces water behind the panels. Some siding makers do not recommend power washing at all, others limit the pressure and cleaners. Use caution around windows, doors, wiring and plumbing. (Source: https://www.vinylsiding.org/vsi-resources/cleaning-and-maintenance/)
- **Stucco:** high pressure can damage stucco's porous texture, cause cracks and chips, and push water behind the wall. Lowe's guidance favors a garden hose and soft brush. (Source: https://www.lowes.com/n/how-to/how-to-clean-stucco). No stucco trade association statement was found: **unverified** at the association level.
- **Windows and seals:** high-pressure spray can force water past window and door seals, break insulated glass seals (leading to fogging), and strip caulk. A window retailer advises never pressure washing windows. (Source: https://www.windowworldclarksville.com/article/why-you-should-never-pressure-wash-your-windows) **(lower confidence: retailer blog)**. A claimed AAMA (window association) recommendation was not verified: **unverified**.

## 6. Concrete

- **Etching:** Concrete Network says a power washer runs at roughly 1,500 to 3,300 psi, more than 50 times the force of a garden hose with a sprayer, and that too high a setting or too close a range can etch the concrete. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/concrete/concrete_driveways/clean.html)
- **Wand marks / "zebra striping" and exposed aggregate:** too much pressure, too narrow a spray, or a wand held too close can strip the cement paste, leave striped troughs and expose aggregate. Removing them can require grinding. Even surface cleaners (rotary attachments) spread pressure evenly. (Source: https://www.blueridgeexteriorcleaning.com/blog/can-pressure-washing-damage-concrete/) **(lower confidence: industry blog; no PCA statement found, so PCA position unverified)**
- **Sealer:** Concrete Network says resealing a driveway every few years restores appearance and makes it less vulnerable to stains, and that cleaned concrete should dry at least 2 to 3 days before resealing so moisture is not trapped. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/concrete/concrete_driveways/clean.html) (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/concrete/concrete_driveways/how-to-maintain.html). Whether pressure washing degrades existing sealers: Concrete Network's stamped concrete guide covers cleaning and resealing (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/stamped-concrete/protect-maintain.html). Specific sealer-damage claims are **unverified**.
- **Efflorescence:** the white powdery deposit on concrete comes from moisture moving through the slab and carrying soluble (calcium) salts to the surface, where they react with CO2 in the air to form calcium carbonate. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/doug_bannister/efflorescence.htm) (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/fix-concrete-color/preventing-efflorescence.html)
- **Rust stains:** common sources include irrigation from wells or sprinkler systems with high iron content, and iron-containing fertilizers (iron sulfate). Oxalic acid is the classic rust remover for concrete. The Portland Cement Association is cited as recommending it. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/fix-concrete-cleaning/remove-rust-stains.html) (Source: https://ask.extension.org/kb/faq.php?id=591530, Ask Extension). Do not publish mixing amounts.
- **Oil stains:** options include strong detergents, concrete degreasers, poultices and microbial (oil-eating) cleaners. Concrete Network notes that typical degreasers loosen oil but don't break it down, so old, heavy stains may only lighten. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/fix-concrete-cleaning/removing-embedded-oil.html) (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/fix-concrete-cleaning/)

## 7. Wood fences and decks

- **Graying:** USDA Forest Products Laboratory: weathering is photo-oxidation of the wood surface driven by UV in sunlight, plus rain washing and repeated swelling and shrinking. The surface becomes rich in cellulose and can take on a silvery-gray sheen. Cycles of wetting and drying roughen the surface and raise the grain. (Source: https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplgtr/fplgtr113/ch15.pdf, Wood Handbook ch. 15, Finishing of Wood) (Source: https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/22122, "Weathering of wood")
- FPL: without maintenance, decks develop checks and cracks, raised grain and mildew, and refinishing reduces these problems. (Source: https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/32439.pdf, "Finishes for Wood Decks") (Source: https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/usda/ah647.pdf, "Finishing Wood Exteriors," Agriculture Handbook 647)
- **Furring / raised grain from pressure:** too much pressure or too tight a spray tears the soft earlywood fibers, leaving a fuzzy surface that must be sanded before stain will take. (Source: https://www.deckstainhelp.com/wood-fuzzies-furring-on-deck/) (Source: https://www.cabotstain.com/content/dam/cbg-cabot/pro/pdfs/cabot-2008-tech-bulletin-3.pdf, Cabot Technical Bulletin #3 "Deck Problems") **(Cabot bulletin content not read directly; partly verified)**
- **Stain makers' cleaning approach:** Behr's deck prep steps are sweep, wet the deck, apply a wood cleaner, scrub with the grain using a stiff brush, rinse thoroughly, and let it dry completely (about 24 hours) before staining. For fences, Behr says that if a pressure washer is used to rinse, use the broadest spray pattern and keep the nozzle at least 24 inches from the surface. (Source: https://www.behr.com/consumer/how-to/stains/how-to-prep-a-deck) (Source: https://www.behr.com/how-to/stains/how-to-stain-a-deck)
- **Drying time before stain:** Sherwin-Williams: allow about 24 to 48 hours after cleaning before staining. Behr: let it dry completely, which may take about 24 hours. (Source: https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/project-center/stain/how-to-prepare-deck-stain) (Source: https://www.sherwin-williams.com/en-us/project-center/stain/how-to-plan-deck-staining) (Source: https://www.behr.com/how-to/stains/how-to-stain-a-deck). In Houston humidity, longer drying is plausible, but that is **unverified** as a sourced statement.
- **Pressure-treated wood maker:** YellaWood's care page says a mild soap and low-pressure washing with a wide fan, keeping the wand moving, will clean surface mold and dirt without damaging the deck. (Source: https://www.yellawood.com/resources/product-education/care-maintenance/)

## 8. Gutters

- "Tiger stripes" (vertical black streaks on the outside face of gutters) are widely described as residue carried by water spilling over the gutter lip (dirt, pollution and asphalt shingle residue) that bonds to the painted aluminum, often called an electrostatic bond. This is why a plain rinse doesn't remove them and a dedicated gutter brightener or hand cleaning is used. (Source: https://www.gutterworks.com/tips-and-resources/how-to-remove-black-streaks-from-gutters) (Source: https://jracenstein.com/expert-advice-learning/how-do-i-remove-those-ugly-black-stripes-on-gutters-1) **(lower confidence: gutter installer and equipment supplier; no manufacturer or university source found; mechanism unverified)**
- Safe site wording: "Those dark vertical stripes on the outside of gutters are grime that bonds to the gutter's painted finish as water spills over the edge. They usually need a gutter-specific cleaner and hand detail, not more pressure."

## 9. Plants, runoff and weeds

- Sodium hypochlorite (the active ingredient in most soft wash solutions) can burn leaves and harm plants at strength. Standard professional practice is to soak plants and soil with water before and after application, cover delicate plants, and rinse thoroughly afterward so plants take up less solution. (Source: https://jracenstein.com/expert-advice-learning/how-to-protect-plants-when-soft-washing-1) (Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_washing) **(lower confidence: equipment supplier; no extension source found)**
- Pressure washing removes the visible part of weeds in cracks and joints but not the roots, so they usually grow back. High pressure can also blow out joint sand between pavers. (Source: https://www.hunker.com/2009363/pressure-washer-kill-weeds-sidewalk-cracks/) (Source: https://www.installitdirect.com/learn/weeds-between-pavers/) **(lower confidence: consumer publisher)**

## 11. Frequency

- Angi: wash a house about once a year. Twice a year may make sense where there is heavy rain, humidity or pollution. (Source: https://www.angi.com/articles/how-often-pressure-wash-house.htm)
- James Hardie: wash fiber cement siding every 6 to 12 months. (Source: https://www.jameshardie.com/blog/siding-durability/tips-for-cleaning-your-siding/)
- Roofs: Angi suggests most homes benefit from roof cleaning every one to three years depending on roof type, climate and tree cover, with more frequent cleaning in humid climates. (Source: https://www.angi.com/articles/pressure-clean-roof-cost.htm)
- Driveway sealing every few years: Concrete Network. (Source: https://www.concretenetwork.com/concrete/concrete_driveways/how-to-maintain.html)

## 13. "Power washing" vs "pressure washing"

- Angi: the commonly cited difference is that power washers use heated water and pressure washers use unheated water. Heat helps break down grease, oil and mildew, so power washing suits tough stains on durable concrete and stone. Heat can harm wood, vinyl and aluminum siding. (Source: https://www.angi.com/articles/power-vs-pressure-washer.htm)
- Equipment makers (Kärcher, Hotsy) explain hot water's advantage: cold water mostly moves grease around, while hot water melts and emulsifies oils and fats, speeds cleaning and needs less detergent. Kärcher says each 50 °F rise roughly halves reaction time. (Source: https://www.kaercher.com/us/commercial/industry-solutions/advantages-of-hot-water-pressure-washers.html) (Source: https://www.hotsy.com/en/resources/media-library/articles/when-to-choose-a-hot-or-cold-water-pressure-washer.html)
- In everyday use, homeowners and many companies use the two terms interchangeably. Treat the hot/cold distinction as a convention used by some sources, not a formal standard. (No standards body definition found: **unverified** as an official definition.)
