The short answer, and why it is a range

Most residential exterior cleaning is priced per square foot, and across the industry the usual band is roughly 15 to 45 cents a square foot, with concrete flatwork at the lower end and delicate or high-access surfaces at the upper end. In practice that puts a typical single job somewhere between $200 and $600, and a whole-house-plus-driveway package between $400 and $900.

Those are ranges, not quotes. Anyone who gives you a firm price for your house without knowing its size, its storeys and its condition is quoting a different house.

Why two quotes for the same house can differ by half

Almost always scope rather than price: one quote is the driveway, the other is the driveway, apron, walkway and porch, and both said "driveway". How to get quotes that are genuinely comparable is covered on the quote page.

What to have ready when you call

You will get a far more accurate number in one call if you have these to hand.

  • Rough square footage of the driveway and the house footprint. Your county appraisal record has the house square footage on it.
  • How many storeys, and whether there is anything unusual to reach.
  • What the staining actually is — green and slick is algae, orange fanning out from a sprinkler is rust, dark and greasy is oil.
  • Any deadline, such as an HOA letter or a listing date.

Where the money is usually well spent

Driveway and walkway cleaning is the cheapest visible improvement available to most houses. A house wash costs more and lasts longer. Roof cleaning costs the most per square foot because of the care and the access, and it is the one where doing it badly shortens the life of something expensive.

If the budget is limited, most people get the most visible return from the flatwork at the front of the house, then the wall that faces north.
