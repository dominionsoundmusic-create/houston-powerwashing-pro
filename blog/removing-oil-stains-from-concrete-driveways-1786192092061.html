How to Remove Oil Stains from Concrete Driveways: A Houston Homeowner's Guide

If you've ever parked a car, changed your oil, or dealt with a leaky engine in your driveway, you already know how stubborn oil stains on concrete can be. Here in Houston, where heat and humidity make everything a little more intense, those dark, greasy patches can bake right into your driveway surface — making them even harder to lift. Whether you're dealing with a fresh drip or a years-old set-in stain, this guide walks you through the most effective methods for removing oil stains from concrete driveways, so you can get your curb appeal back without the frustration.

Why Oil Stains Are So Difficult to Remove from Concrete

Concrete looks solid, but it's actually a porous material. Motor oil, transmission fluid, and other petroleum-based liquids seep into those tiny pores quickly — especially in Houston's scorching summer heat, which essentially drives the oil deeper into the slab. Once it penetrates below the surface, simple scrubbing with soap and water won't cut it.

On top of that, oxidized oil (old, dried stains) chemically bonds with the concrete over time. This is why a stain that looks manageable on the surface may actually extend a quarter inch or more into the concrete. Understanding this helps explain why homeowners often need multiple treatment steps — or professional power washing equipment — to fully restore their driveway.

Step 1 — Act Fast on Fresh Oil Stains

Speed is your best friend when it comes to fresh oil spills. The longer oil sits on concrete, the deeper it soaks in. Here's what to do immediately after a spill:

  • Absorb the excess: Pour cat litter, baking soda, cornstarch, or commercial oil absorbent generously over the wet stain. Let it sit for at least 30 minutes — longer for larger spills.
  • Sweep it up: Collect all the absorbent material and dispose of it properly.
  • Apply dish soap: Squirt a generous amount of grease-cutting dish detergent (like Dawn) directly onto the stain. Add a small amount of hot water and scrub vigorously with a stiff-bristle brush for several minutes.
  • Rinse thoroughly: Use a garden hose or, better yet, a pressure washer to rinse away the loosened oil and soap.

Acting quickly can mean the difference between a stain that disappears after one treatment and one that requires repeated effort. In Houston's summer heat, don't let a spill sit overnight if you can help it.

Step 2 — Treating Set-In and Older Oil Stains

Old oil stains require a more aggressive approach. Here are the most effective DIY methods Houston homeowners can try before calling in the pros:

  • Degreaser or concrete cleaner: Products like Simple Green, Zep Heavy-Duty Degreaser, or specialty concrete degreasers available at hardware stores can break down old oil. Apply, let dwell for 10–15 minutes, scrub hard, and rinse.
  • Trisodium Phosphate (TSP): A powerful cleaning agent that works well on petroleum stains. Mix about half a cup with a gallon of hot water, apply to the stain, scrub, and rinse. Use gloves and eye protection — TSP is caustic.
  • Poultice method: For deep stains, mix an absorbent powder (like diatomaceous earth or powdered detergent) with a solvent (acetone or paint thinner) to form a thick paste. Spread it over the stain, cover with plastic wrap, tape the edges, and let it sit for 24 hours. The paste draws the oil out of the concrete as it dries. Scrape it up and rinse clean.
  • Baking soda paste: A more eco-friendly option — mix baking soda with water to create a thick paste, apply it, let it dry, and scrub away. Best for lighter or more recent stains.

Keep in mind that older or larger stains may require several rounds of treatment. Patience and repetition are key.

Why Pressure Washing Makes Such a Big Difference

DIY methods can work well for small, relatively fresh stains, but there's a reason professional power washing is the gold standard for driveway oil stain removal. A commercial-grade pressure washer operates at 3,000–4,000 PSI or higher, compared to the 1,500–2,000 PSI of a typical consumer unit. That extra pressure — combined with hot water and professional-grade degreasers — does something home methods simply can't: it physically forces the oil and cleaning solution out of the pores of the concrete rather than just cleaning the surface.

Here in Houston, professional power washing services use heated water units that are especially effective at emulsifying petroleum-based stains. Hot water softens the oil, while the high-pressure stream blasts it out from below the surface. The result is a dramatically cleaner driveway — often restoring the concrete close to its original color without the risk of surface damage from improper technique or incorrect nozzle use.

Beyond oil stains, a professional power wash removes Houston's notorious mildew, algae, rust streaks, and general grime that build up in our humid climate — making your entire driveway look refreshed and well-maintained.

Mistakes to Avoid When Removing Oil Stains

A few common mistakes can make the problem worse or damage your driveway surface:

  • Using bleach alone: Bleach is great for mold and mildew but it does very little to break down petroleum-based oil. It may lighten the stain temporarily without actually removing it.
  • Scrubbing too aggressively with wire brushes: Wire bristles can scratch and pit the surface of your concrete, creating even more porous areas where future stains can penetrate.
  • Skipping the rinse: Leaving degreaser or TSP residue on your driveway can attract more dirt and discolor the concrete over time. Always rinse thoroughly.
  • Using the wrong pressure washer nozzle: A zero-degree nozzle at close range can etch or crack concrete. Use a 25-degree or 40-degree nozzle, or let a professional handle it.
  • Ignoring the problem: The longer you wait, the more deeply the oil penetrates and the harder it is to remove. What's treatable today may require grinding or resurfacing if left for years.

Some oil stains are simply beyond DIY treatment — and that's okay. If you've tried multiple cleaning methods and the stain persists, if you have multiple large stains across your driveway, or if you simply want fast, reliable results without the elbow grease, it's time to call a professional.
