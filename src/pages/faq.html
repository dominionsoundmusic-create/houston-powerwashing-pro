" - q: "How do pressure washing companies charge?" a: "

Usually by surface and size: square feet of concrete, the size and height of the house, the area of the roof, the length of fence. Heavy mildew, oil or rust and hard access add to it, and many companies have a minimum. The cost guide shows published ranges and what moves them.

" - q: "How often should I pressure wash my house?" a: "

In Houston's humidity, many homes need a gentle house wash about once a year, and shaded or tree-covered houses sooner. Siding makers such as James Hardie suggest washing every 6 to 12 months. Houses should be soft washed, not blasted. See how often to wash a house.

" - q: "How often should a driveway be pressure washed?" a: "

There is no fixed schedule. Watch for gray or green film in shady spots, black tire marks and a dark band along the lawn edge. Sunny driveways can go much longer than shaded ones. Driveway cleaning explains what to look for.

" - q: "How long does pressure washing take?" a: "

A driveway and walk is often a morning's work. A full house wash, or a house plus concrete and fence, can take most of a day. Stains that need pre-treatment add time. The company can estimate once it knows the size.

" - q: "How long does a pressure washing job last?" a: "

It depends on shade, moisture and what grows nearby. Sunny concrete and siding can stay clean for a year or more, while surfaces under trees or beside sprinklers can darken again within months in Houston's climate.

" - q: "Will pressure washing kill my plants?" a: "

Plain water will not, though high pressure can shred leaves. The cleaning solution used in soft washing can burn plants at strength, so a careful company soaks plants with water before and after, covers delicate ones and rinses well. Ask how the company protects landscaping.

" - q: "Does pressure washing kill weeds?" a: "

It removes the top growth in cracks and paver joints but not the roots, so weeds usually come back. Too much pressure can also wash out paver joint sand.

" - q: "Does power washing damage wood?" a: "

It can. Too much pressure tears the soft fibers of fences and decks, leaving a fuzzy surface that has to be sanded before it will take stain. Wood should be cleaned with low pressure or a soft wash and left to dry before staining. See fence cleaning and deck cleaning.

" - q: "What kind of pressure washer should I use on siding?" a: "

The safer answer is not to point a pressure washer at siding at all. James Hardie says not to use a high-pressure washer on its fiber cement siding, and the Vinyl Siding Institute warns that spraying upward forces water behind panels. Hire a company that soft washes siding. See house washing.

" - q: "Can a roof be pressure washed?" a: "

Asphalt shingle makers and their trade association say no: high pressure strips the protective granules. The black streaks are algae, which a low-pressure soft wash treats. See roof cleaning.

" - q: "What is commercial pressure washing?" a: "

Cleaning for business properties: storefront sidewalks, entrances, dumpster pads, drive-thrus, parking areas and building exteriors. It often involves grease and stricter rules about wash water. See commercial pressure washing.

" - q: "Are pressure washing companies licensed in Texas?" a: "

Pressure washing is not one of the trades licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. In the City of Houston, the Health Department issues a pressure washer permit for companies that wash grease and special waste areas. For any job, ask for proof of liability insurance and a written price.

Yes. Use the form on the contact page or at the bottom of any page. A local company will call you back.
