Houston Power Washing Pro takes calls from Houston itself and from the suburbs that ring it, north to Conroe, west to Katy, southwest to Missouri City and south toward Clear Lake. The number and the process are the same everywhere: an automated assistant answers, takes down the job and the location, and puts you through to an independent, owner-run local pressure washing company.

There are no branch offices. What changes from one area to the next is the job itself. A wooded lot in Kingwood grows mildew differently from a new-build driveway in Cypress. A Woodlands home sits under township covenants, while a house in Pearland answers to city code and its HOA. The pages below cover those local details so you know what to expect and what to ask.

Houston inside the city limits

The line covers the City of Houston too, from the Heights and Montrose to Meyerland, Clear Lake and Kingwood (which is part of the city). Inside city limits, one rule matters for any outdoor cleaning: the City of Houston's storm water ordinance makes it an offense to let anything other than storm water into the storm sewer, apart from a short list of exceptions. A careful company plans where its wash water goes, especially on commercial jobs.

Businesses that wash grease areas or dumpster pads inside the city have extra rules. The Houston Health Department issues a pressure washer permit for that kind of work, so ask the company you are connected with whether it holds one if your job involves grease.

How coverage works

  1. You say where the property is. The assistant asks for the city or ZIP code along with what needs cleaning.
  2. You are put through. The call goes to an independent local pressure washing company, which hears that summary first.
  3. The company confirms it can do the job. It works out travel and timing with you directly. If it is on a job, it calls you back.
