Friendswood began in 1895 as a Quaker colony on 1,538 acres at the headwaters of Clear Creek, planted with figs, Satsuma oranges and rice. Much has changed since then, but the trees and the creeks still shape how a Friendswood home weathers, and that is the starting point for pressure washing in Friendswood, TX.

Shade, creeks and slow-drying surfaces in Friendswood

Clear Creek, Cowart Creek and Mary's Creek all wind through town, and mature trees shade a lot of its neighborhoods. That makes for beautiful streets and damp surfaces. A fence under a canopy may only see direct sun for an hour or two, and a north-facing brick wall can stay wet for days after a shower.

Wherever things stay wet, growth follows. Expect green film on shaded concrete, black spotting on fence boards, tea-colored leaf stains where wet leaves sat on the driveway, and dark streaks below the roofline. These are cleaning problems, not repair problems, as long as the method suits the surface.

Three kinds of Friendswood property, three different jobs

Large wooded lots

Deed-restricted areas such as The Forest of Friendswood have big lots and mature trees, with an architectural review committee that covers landscaping, fencing and upkeep. Long driveways, long fence runs and roofs under branches are the usual list.

Established subdivisions

Wedgewood Village, one of the oldest subdivisions in the city, has more than 900 homes and an HOA that enforces its deed restrictions. Older brick, painted trim and settled walks call for a lighter touch than new construction.

Homes near the creeks

All three creeks left their banks during Hurricane Harvey in 2017. Homes close to them can carry silt on slabs and a faint line on fences or garage doors long after the water drops.

Protecting trees and beds while the work is done

What you want to see

Wash water and Friendswood's storm drains

Friendswood sits in both Galveston and Harris counties, but one city stormwater code covers every address. Chapter 86, Article V lists wash water among the non-stormwater discharges that do not belong in the storm drain system, and the city runs a Stormwater Management Program under its state permit. For a homeowner, the practical point is to ask before the work starts how the company will keep rinse water and cleaning products out of the inlet at the curb.

Getting a wooded Friendswood lot ready for the visit

A little preparation makes the quote more accurate and the work go smoother, especially where trees drop leaves and limbs all year.

  • Note the shady spots. Tell the company which stretches of fence, walk or wall stay dark, so it brings enough time for them.
  • Mention old finishes. If the fence was stained years ago or the trim paint is chalky, say so; both change how gently the surface should be treated.
  • Clear what you can. Move pots, toys and patio furniture off the concrete, and unlock any side gates the crew will need.
  • Ask about an outside spigot. The company may ask to use your outdoor water supply, so check that the hose bib works and is easy to reach.
