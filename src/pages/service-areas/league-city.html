Pressure washing in League City, TX, starts with where your house sits. A brick bungalow under the Butler Oaks on East Main, a waterfront home near Clear Lake and a newer two-story in a lake community each get dirty in their own way, and each calls for a slightly different approach.

What League City weather does to a house

League City sits on the south side of Clear Lake, with Clear Creek along its northern edge and Galveston Bay a short drive east. Humid Gulf air keeps north walls, shaded walks and the underside of eaves damp for long stretches, and that is where green algae and black mildew settle first. Summer storms then splash soil up the bottom row of brick and coat driveways with grit.

The result is familiar on almost any street in town: a dull gray driveway with a darker band near the lawn, a fence that has gone from tan to charcoal, and streaks running down from the gutters. None of it is a sign of neglect. It is what this climate does to porous surfaces, and a good company knows how to lift it without hurting the material underneath.

League City at a glance

  • 114,392 residents at the 2020 census, mostly in Galveston County with a small piece in Harris County north of Clear Creek.
  • Two watersheds drain the city: Clear Creek and Dickinson Bayou, both headed toward Clear Lake and Galveston Bay.
  • Butler Oaks planted in 1907 still shade Main Street and nearby blocks in the Historic District.
  • Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and Harvey in 2017 flooded thousands of homes here.

The services League City homeowners ask for most

  • House washing and soft washing. The shaded side of a League City house is often the first thing a neighbor notices. Soft washing uses low pressure and a cleaning solution, which is the safe choice for siding, stucco and older brick.
  • Driveway and sidewalk cleaning. Wide suburban driveways collect tire marks, storm grit and mildew along the lawn edge. A surface cleaner gives an even result without stripes.
  • Fence cleaning. Wood privacy fences gray out quickly in coastal humidity and take the worst of any high water. They need gentle pressure so the boards do not turn fuzzy.
  • Deck and patio cleaning. Decks and pool surrounds near the lake stay damp in shade and grow slick algae, which is a slip risk as well as an eyesore.
  • Roof cleaning. Black streaks on shingles are mostly algae. Shingle makers warn against high-pressure washing, so ask for a soft wash.

Keeping rinse water out of Clear Creek

A storm drain at the curb in League City is not a sewer that goes to a treatment plant. It feeds the creek and bayou system that ends in Clear Lake and Galveston Bay. The city's Clean Water ordinance, Chapter 43 of its code, prohibits discharges to the storm sewer that do not belong there, and the city's stormwater page asks residents to report illicit discharges and marks drains to discourage dumping.

That gives you a simple test for any company: ask how it handles the runoff. A careful crew blocks or covers nearby inlets, soaks the lawn and beds before and after any cleaning solution, and never rinses oil or soap straight into the gutter. It should be able to explain its method without hesitation.

After a flood or a named storm

Harvey sent Clear Creek far above flood stage in 2017 and flooded a large share of League City homes. After the post-Katrina and post-Ike map updates, thousands more homes were listed in the 100-year and 500-year floodplains. For many homes, a wash makes sense as one of the last steps of storm recovery.

  1. Let repairs come first. Washing should wait until drywall, framing and electrical work are done or scheduled, so nothing gets cleaned twice.
  2. Describe the water line. Tell the company how high the water came on the fence, garage door or brick. That shapes the plan and the price.
  3. Ask about the soil. Silt left on concrete can stain if it bakes in, and a company may pre-wet or pre-treat it before the main rinse.
  4. Keep the invoice. A dated invoice is useful for your own records, an HOA, or an insurance conversation you are already having.

Watering stages and timing

League City buys most of its drinking water from the City of Houston's Southeast Water Purification Plant and the Gulf Coast Water Authority. Its Drought Contingency Plan begins with a Stage 1 request to avoid outdoor watering from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. and to water twice a week on your trash days, and later stages can make limits mandatory. If a stage is in effect when you call, ask the company how it plans around it.
