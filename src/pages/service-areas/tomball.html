Check Tomball's drought stage before you book

Of all the rules that touch pressure washing here, the drought plan matters most. Tomball runs its own water system on groundwater, and when demand climbs it moves through stages that limit outdoor water use. In the 2023 drought it was the first city in Southeast Texas to make restrictions mandatory.

  1. Look up the current stage. The city's Drought Status page shows it. Stages are triggered when demand reaches set shares of production (60 percent for Stage 1, 70 percent for Stage 2, and up from there) for three days in a row.
  2. Stage 1: go easy. Residents are asked to voluntarily limit power washing. A job can still happen, but it is a good time to keep it to what really needs doing.
  3. Stage 2 or higher: wait. Washing sidewalks, driveways, parking lots and other hard surfaces is banned. So is washing buildings (other than for fire protection) and flushing gutters.
  4. Plan for the lawn too. Normal watering is allowed only between 7 p.m. and 5 a.m., with even addresses on Sunday and Thursday and odd addresses on Saturday and Wednesday. Avoid scheduling a wash for the morning after your watering night if the yard tends to stay soggy.

If the stage changes after you book, talk to the company you are working with about a new date. That is a normal conversation in a dry summer.

Pressure washing for Tomball's mix of old and new

Tomball was platted in 1907 on the Trinity and Brazos Valley Railway. That December the town of Peck was renamed for Thomas Ball, the former congressman credited with routing the line through. The original 1907 depot still stands as the Tomball Depot Museum at 201 S. Elm St., and the Tomball Museum Center keeps about a dozen restored historic buildings. Old brick, old mortar and old paint all need a light touch.

Outside the historic core, the picture changes. Wildwood at Northpointe and Villages of Northpointe are established planned neighborhoods, and Rosehill Reserve to the west is a newer community of brick and stone homes. Past those, many lots are larger, with long privacy fences, barns and outbuildings that take on gray weathering and green growth.

Two creeks, two floods in a row

The north side of Tomball drains toward Spring Creek and the south side toward Willow Creek. During Harvey in 2017, about 30 inches of rain fell on Tomball and Magnolia over four days, and Willow Creek rose about a foot above its 100-year level. Homes near Spring, Willow, Mill and Walnut creeks flooded only a year after the April and May 2016 floods.

Keeping wash water out of the storm drains

Tomball holds a stormwater permit and runs a Stormwater Management Program built around a simple message: only rain down the storm drain. Rinse water carrying soap, mildew treatment or oil should not be pushed into the street inlet. A good company blocks or diverts runoff, steers it to grass where that is safe, and collects it on commercial jobs. Ask how it handles this before work starts.
