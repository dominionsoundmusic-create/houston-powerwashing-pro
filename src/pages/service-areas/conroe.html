What makes pressure washing in Conroe different

Isaac Conroe, a Houston lumberman, opened a sawmill here in 1881, and the town grew around timber from the surrounding forest. Today Conroe is the Montgomery County seat with close to 90,000 residents in the 2020 census, and Sam Houston National Forest, roughly 163,000 acres of pine and hardwood, sits just to the north. Tall trees mean shade, falling needles, spring pollen and surfaces that stay damp long after a rain.

The other half of the story is the lake. Lake Conroe was built as a water-supply reservoir, and the San Jacinto River Authority treats lake water at a plant by the dam and pipes it to partner utilities. So anything rinsed off a lakefront deck, seawall walk or boathouse roof is going into drinking-water supply, which raises the bar for how a company handles runoff.

Lake Conroe docks, boathouses and waterfront lots

Waterfront property comes with its own paperwork and its own risks. A few facts worth knowing before anyone brings a pressure washer near the water:

  • Permitted structures The San Jacinto River Authority's Lake Conroe rules say no pier, dock, floating boathouse or wharf may be built, operated or maintained on the reservoir without an SJRA permit, and SJRA charges an annual fee for each structure.
  • Where SJRA's reach ends The Authority's jurisdiction runs up to the 201-foot contour, which is the lake's normal pool elevation.
  • What the watershed plan worries about SJRA's Lake Conroe Watershed Protection Plan names stormwater runoff from development around the lake as a main pollution source, with nutrients and bacteria as the listed concerns.
  • How high the water has been During Harvey in 2017 the lake reached about 206.2 feet, above the 1994 record, and the dam released a record flow downstream.

None of that means a dock cannot be cleaned. It means you should ask more questions than you would about a driveway. When you call, say it is a dock or boathouse and ask whether the company handles docks and boathouses at all. If it does, ask:

  • What will go on the wood or composite, and will any of it drip straight into the lake?
  • Can the dock be cleaned with low pressure and plain water, or does the growth need a treatment first?
  • How will the crew protect shoreline plants and keep rinse water on the bank side where possible?
  • Has the company worked on waterfront structures before, and can it describe how it went?

Services Conroe homeowners ask about most

Roof cleaning

Shaded slopes under pines grow the dark streaks that make a roof look older than it is. A soft wash removes them without grinding the shingle surface.

Deck and patio cleaning

Lakeside decks and backyard patios turn green and slick in the shade. Wood needs gentle pressure so the grain does not fuzz up before you stain it.

House washing

North walls tucked behind trees collect mildew. Low-pressure soft washing suits siding, stone veneer and painted trim found on lake homes and new builds alike.

Fence cleaning

Wood privacy fences along wooded lot lines gray out fast. A careful wash brings the color back and gets the boards ready for sealer.

Driveway cleaning

Even a new subdivision driveway darkens where the trees drip and the sprinklers hit. A surface cleaner gives an even result without stripes.

Gutter brightening

Black streaks on the outside of gutters often show up alongside roof algae, and the two are commonly handled in one visit.

Newer subdivisions and association letters

Conroe grew fast enough that the Census Bureau once named it the fastest-growing large city in the country (July 2015 to July 2016). Many of those homes sit in planned communities. Grand Central Park, for example, has a recorded declaration with deed restrictions covering exterior paint, landscaping and fencing, and exterior changes there need approval in advance. Every association words its rules differently, so read yours before you change anything about the outside of the house.

Cleaning is usually not a change. Repainting or staining a fence a new color may be. If a wash is the first step toward a stain or paint job, ask your association first, then book the cleaning.

After high water

Lakefront and West Fork properties that took on water in past floods often carry a faint silt line on brick, fences and slab edges. If you are dealing with that, mention it on the call. A company that sees flood residue regularly will usually want to look before pricing, because silt can hide damage underneath.
