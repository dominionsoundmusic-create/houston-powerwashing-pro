Most Cypress pressure washing calls begin with one of two things: a courtesy letter from the homeowners association, or a slow realization that the driveway, the roof and the north wall have all turned a shade darker since last spring. Cypress is not a city. It is a large stretch of unincorporated Harris County along US 290, roughly 20 miles northwest of downtown Houston, so the rules around your house come from your deed restrictions, your water district and the county.

Why pressure washing in Cypress comes with its own homework

The Texas State Historical Association describes Cypress as more of a region than a single town: over 100 square miles spread across the Cypress Creek and Little Cypress Creek watersheds, with settlers arriving by 1831 and German families farming along the creek in the 1840s. Today much of that ground is subdivisions and master-planned communities, each with its own covenants. Two homes a mile apart can answer to completely different maintenance standards.

The weather does not respect subdivision lines, though. The nearest long-running National Weather Service station, at Bush Intercontinental, records about 52 inches of rain in a normal year. Purdue Extension notes that mildew grows best where air barely moves, such as walls tucked behind shrubs or trees. Think of the strip between two houses, or the back wall behind a hedge. That is where green and black growth shows up first.

The services that matter most in Cypress

Any surface around a Cypress home can be cleaned, but these are the ones that tend to come up on association letters and in conversations with neighbors.

  • Roof cleaning The dark streaks on asphalt shingles are mostly algae, according to the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association, which warns that high pressure can damage shingles. A soft wash is the method to ask for.
  • House washing Brick, stone and painted siding all clean up with low pressure and the right solution, which avoids pushing water behind panels or trim.
  • Driveway cleaning The driveway is the first surface anyone sees from the street, including whoever drives the neighborhood for the association.
  • Gutter brightening Area HOA reminders mention gutters filling with leaves and pine needles, and the black stripes on the outside face need a gutter cleaner and hand work, not more pressure.
  • Fence cleaning Wood fences gray and mildew in shade. Too much pressure leaves fuzzy, raised grain that has to be sanded before stain.

Lakes, creeks and where the rinse water ends up

Water is a big part of what makes Cypress neighborhoods attractive, and it changes how a cleaning job should be planned. Bridgeland, which Howard Hughes has been developing since 2006, covers about 11,400 acres and plans roughly 900 acres of lakes. Those lakes soaked up overflow from Cypress Creek during Hurricane Harvey. Towne Lake, built by Caldwell Companies, is planned around a 300-acre recreational lake.

In communities like these, a storm drain at the curb often leads to water you can see from your back porch. Before work starts on a patio, a pool deck or a lakeside walk, ask three things: what product goes on near the shoreline, whether nearby plants get soaked down before and after, and how the rinse water will be kept out of the inlet.

Cypress Creek itself has a long flood history. Its watershed covers more than 300 square miles of northwest Harris County, and Harvey dropped close to 30 inches of rain on it in 2017. Since the Flood Control District's buyout program began in 1985, more than 13,200 homes in the watershed have flooded. If a fence or a brick wall near the creek still carries a faint waterline, describe it when you call so the company knows what it is looking at.
