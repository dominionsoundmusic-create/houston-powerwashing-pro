Loblolly pine, oak and sweetgum crowd the creeks and older streets here, and they drop needles into gutters, coat decks with pollen and keep roofs and north walls in shade long enough for algae to settle in. A good company deals with all of it gently, because most of what those trees stain is too soft to blast.

Why Spring roofs and siding stay green

Along the Spring Creek Greenway the woods are a mix of loblolly pine, oak, sweetgum and bottomland hardwoods, and neighborhoods such as Spring Creek Forest are known for tall pines on wooded lots. Pine needles settle in roof valleys and gutters, where they hold moisture against the shingles. The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association identifies the black streaks that follow as algae, and warns that high-pressure washing is likely to damage asphalt roofing.

Then comes the yellow season. Houston-area tree pollen runs from about February into April, oak pollen usually peaks in March, and the Houston Health Department often reports very heavy counts during that stretch. Cars, walks, screens and patio furniture all turn yellow, and the pollen mixes with dew to make a film that feeds mildew on shaded surfaces.

No city hall: the county, your MUD and your HOA

Spring is a census-designated place of 62,559 people (2020 census), not a city. It sits mostly in unincorporated northwest Harris County, with a piece in Montgomery County, inside Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction. That changes who writes the rules.

  • Runoff Harris County's stormwater quality regulations apply across its unincorporated areas. Routine pavement washing is allowed only without detergents, other chemicals or heat. The Harris County Flood Control District runs the county's stormwater permit compliance program.
  • Water Your tap is usually fed by a MUD, not a city. Around 143 MUDs joined the North Harris County Regional Water Authority's groundwater reduction plan, which is moving the area toward surface water.
  • Appearance Master-planned communities set the standard. Gleannloch Farms, off Champion Forest Drive, publishes design guidelines section by section and its community association enforces the deed restrictions.
  • Fines A Texas homeowners association generally owes you written notice and a fair window to put a curable issue right before it fines you (Property Code 209.006).

Along Cypress Creek

The south side of Spring borders Cypress Creek, and the creek has flooded hard. More than 8,700 homes along it took on water in Hurricane Harvey in 2017, and about 1,700 flooded the year before in the 2016 Tax Day flood. Ponderosa Forest, Westador, Champion Forest and Wimbledon Champions were among the Spring and Klein neighborhoods hit.

Years later, some fences and brick walls in those areas still carry a tide line of silt. Wood that has soaked in floodwater and then weathered tends to gray and mildew faster. Both need a careful hand: low pressure on brick and mortar, and a gentle wash on wood so the grain is not torn up. Mention the flood history when you call so the company can plan for it.

Old Town Spring and older buildings

Spring was settled by German families around 1840, the railroad came through in 1871, and by the early 1900s it was a railroad town. Old Town Spring, near Spring-Cypress and Hardy roads, keeps that era alive as a restored village of roughly 150 shops in older buildings and cottages.

Aged wood siding, old paint and soft mortar are exactly where a pressure washer does the most damage. If you own or run a shop there, look for a company that washes these surfaces at low pressure and can talk through timing around your business hours.
