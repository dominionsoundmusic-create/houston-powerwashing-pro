Driveway cleaning in Houston is one of the quickest ways to change how a house looks from the street. Concrete is porous, and in a climate this wet it soaks up whatever lands on it: mildew in the shade, rubber from tires, iron from sprinkler water, oil from the car. A professional cleaning lifts most of that out evenly, without the striped, patchy look a rushed job leaves behind.

What is actually on your driveway

Not every dark spot is dirt, and each kind of stain calls for a different treatment. Knowing which ones you have makes the phone call quicker and the quote more accurate.

Driveway pressure washing in Houston, done evenly

The difference between a good driveway job and a poor one shows up in the light the next morning. A careful company cleans flat concrete with a rotary surface cleaner, a round attachment with spinning arms underneath that keeps the spray a fixed distance from the slab. That spreads the force evenly, so there are no light and dark bands. Edges, steps and the strip along the garage door get a hand wand afterward.

Concrete Network points out that a pressure washer can put out more than 50 times the force of a garden hose, and that too much of it, or holding the spray too close, can etch the surface and expose the stone underneath. That is why you hire someone who does this every week rather than learning on your own slab.

What you want to see

  • A surface cleaner used across the open concrete
  • Stains pointed out and treated before the main wash
  • Lawn edges and beds rinsed so treatment does not linger
  • Storm drain inlets near the curb blocked or protected
  • A final rinse that leaves no grime pooled on the walk

What should make you pause

  • A single narrow wand used for the whole driveway
  • Sweeping strokes that leave visible lines as it dries
  • Oil and treatment washed down the gutter into the street
  • No mention of rust or oil when you pointed them out
  • A price that changes once the work has started

Sidewalks, front walks and the curb strip

The public sidewalk in front of your house and the walk to your front door usually get cleaned in the same visit as the driveway. They are narrower and often shaded by street trees, so they tend to be greener and more slippery than the driveway itself. In many neighborhoods, the strip of sidewalk crossing the driveway apron is what a deed restriction inspector sees first from the car.

Tell the company about steps, handrails, a brick border or pavers when you call. Pavers need a gentler approach so the sand between them stays in place.

Drought rules and driveway washing

Houston summers can be dry as well as humid, and several cities around Houston limit washing pavement once their drought plans move up a stage. If you are in one of these places, check the current stage before you book.

  • Tomball: Stage 2 bans washing sidewalks, driveways and other hard surfaces. Tomball was the first Southeast Texas city to go to mandatory restrictions in the 2023 drought.
  • Conroe: under mandatory Stage 2 rules, washing sidewalks, driveways and buildings is prohibited.
  • Missouri City: the 2025 drought plan prohibits using water for pavement washing in Stages 3 and 4.
  • Sugar Land: its drought plan counts washing down driveways and sidewalks as non-essential water use.

These rules change from year to year, so treat the city's own announcement as the final word.

Where the wash water goes

Driveways slope to the street, and the street drains to the bayous. Inside the City of Houston, Chapter 47 of the city code makes it an offense to send anything into the storm sewer system that is not made up entirely of rain water. A responsible company plans for that, especially when it is treating oil, using a degreaser or cleaning a driveway right next to a curb inlet. Ask how it keeps runoff out of the drain.

After the cleaning: sealing and keeping it clean

A clean driveway is the right starting point for a sealer. Concrete Network recommends resealing every few years to help the surface resist stains and suggests letting cleaned concrete dry for at least 2 to 3 days before any sealer goes on. Between cleanings, a few habits help:

  • Aim sprinklers at the lawn, not the concrete, to cut down on rust fans and damp edges.
  • Put a drip pan under a leaking car until it is fixed, since fresh oil is much easier to deal with than old oil.
  • Trim shrubs and low branches that keep the edges of the slab in shade.
  • Blow off pollen and leaves in spring before rain presses them into the surface.
