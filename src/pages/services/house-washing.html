House washing in Houston is less about dirt and more about biology. The film that turns a north wall green or leaves black trails under the gutters is living growth, mostly mildew and algae, and it comes back every humid season. Soft washing kills it at the root with a cleaning solution and rinses it off gently, so the siding, paint and seals around the windows come through intact.

Why Houston houses turn green

  • Wet air Average morning humidity in Houston sits close to 90 percent. Kansas State extension material says mold grows readily once relative humidity passes about 70 percent.
  • Warm months Molds do best roughly between 77 and 86 degrees Fahrenheit, which covers much of a Houston year.
  • Rain National Weather Service normals put Bush Intercontinental at about 52 inches a year, so walls rarely stay dry for long.
  • Still air and shade Purdue Extension notes mildew is most common on walls behind trees and shrubs, where air cannot move.

What is soft pressure washing?

People search the phrase "soft pressure washing" because they have heard both terms and are not sure how they differ. Here is the short version. Pressure washing relies on force: a tight, fast stream of water knocks grime loose. Soft washing relies on chemistry: a cleaning solution goes on at low pressure, sits long enough to kill mildew, algae and lichen, then gets rinsed away at about the strength of a garden hose.

Angi describes soft washing as the method for delicate surfaces such as painted wood, vinyl, stucco, brick and roofing, and pressure washing as the tool for hard surfaces like concrete. That split matters on a house, because most of what you see from the street is the delicate kind. A house wash done the right way is mostly soft washing, with higher pressure saved for the driveway and walks.

There is a second benefit. Blasting a green wall clean with water alone removes what you can see but leaves the roots of the growth in the texture of the siding, so the color creeps back quickly. A solution that actually kills the growth tends to keep the wall cleaner for longer.

How a soft wash house cleaning usually goes

  1. A walk around first. The company notes the siding type, loose boards, cracked stucco, failing caulk and anything fragile near the walls.
  2. Plants get protected. Beds and shrubs along the foundation are soaked with plain water, and delicate plants may be covered.
  3. Solution goes on low and slow. It is applied from the bottom of each wall upward so it does not streak, and given time to work.
  4. A gentle rinse top to bottom. Windows, soffits and trim are rinsed along with the walls, and the plants get another soaking.
  5. A final look together. Any missed spot under an eave or behind the air conditioning unit is easier to fix while the crew is still there.

Got a deed restriction letter about mildew?

You are far from alone. Sugar Land's largest association, First Colony, publishes a Top Ten of the deed restriction problems it cites most often, and mildew, mold and staining on houses, fences and driveways made that list. In Pearland, the recorded architectural guidelines for Shadow Creek Ranch require any paint used on stucco to contain a mildew retardant, which tells you how seriously associations there take the problem.

Texas Property Code section 209.006 generally requires an association to send written notice by certified mail and give a reasonable chance to fix a curable violation before it can fine you. If you have a letter in hand, mention the deadline when you call so the company you are connected with knows the timing matters.

House washing questions worth asking before you say yes

The company you speak with sets its own price and its own method, so a few direct questions will tell you a lot.

  • "Will you soft wash the siding, or use high pressure?" Anything other than soft washing on vinyl, fiber cement, stucco or painted wood deserves a follow-up question.
  • "What do you do to protect my plants?" Listen for pre-soaking, covering delicate beds and a thorough rinse afterward.
  • "Is the price for the whole house, or wall by wall?" Find out whether gutters, the porch ceiling, the fence or the driveway are included or extra.
  • "Can I see proof of liability insurance?" Water forced behind a panel or into a light fixture is rare with a careful crew, but you want coverage if it happens.

Houses near tall trees need more attention

A house under heavy canopy stays damp longer after every rain. The Woodlands is the clearest example. Its 2025 forestry management plan lists pines and oaks overhead with magnolia, dogwood and crepe myrtle in the middle layer, so many lots get very little direct sun on the walls. Homes there tend to grow mildew on shaded walls and collect pollen on siding each spring.
