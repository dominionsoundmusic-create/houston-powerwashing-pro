What residential pressure washing covers

"Pressure washing" means cleaning with water at much higher pressure than a garden hose. It is the right tool for hard, durable surfaces. Softer materials like siding, shingles and painted wood need a gentler method called soft washing, which uses low pressure and a cleaning solution instead of force. A good company switches between the two on the same property.

Why Houston concrete gets dirty so fast

Bush Intercontinental averages roughly 52 inches of rain in a year, according to National Weather Service climate normals, and morning humidity in Houston regularly sits around 90 percent. Mildew and algae thrive in exactly that kind of air, especially on surfaces that stay damp: the north side of the house, the strip of walk beside the flower bed, the driveway under a tree.

Spring adds a layer of yellow oak pollen, usually peaking in March. Summer storms splash soil onto slabs. Sprinklers fed by water with iron in it leave orange rings. By the time a driveway looks dark, it is usually a mix of all of these.

Who calls about pressure washing

  • Homeowners who got an HOA letter. Many Houston-area associations write up dark driveways and green walks. A clean surface and a dated receipt usually close the matter.
  • People getting ready to sell or rent. Clean concrete makes listing photos look better.
  • Hosts before a big weekend. Patios and pool decks get slippery when algae builds up in shade.
  • Anyone planning to seal or stain. Sealer and stain need clean, fully dry surfaces to bond.
  • Homeowners after storm season. Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and storms leave silt, leaf stains and debris behind.

What a careful pressure washing company does

You do not need to know how the machines work. You only need to know what good work looks like, so you can tell whether the company you hire is doing it.

Signs of a careful company

  • Uses a rotary surface cleaner on flat concrete so the pressure is even and there are no stripes.
  • Pre-treats mildew, oil and rust instead of trying to blast them out with pressure alone.
  • Soaks plants and grass before and after any cleaning solution goes on.
  • Keeps wash water out of storm drains. City of Houston code treats anything other than storm water going into the storm sewer as an offense, with few exceptions.
  • Switches to soft washing for siding, shingles, wood and painted surfaces.
  • Walks the job with you at the end.

Warning signs

  • Plans to use the same high pressure on everything, including siding and the roof.
  • Holds a narrow spray close to concrete, leaving light and dark lines.
  • Will not put the price per surface in writing.
  • Cannot show proof of liability insurance when asked.
  • Lets cleaning solution and grime run straight into the street drain.

Three questions to ask any pressure washing company

Pressure washing is not one of the trades licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, so there is no state license number to look up. These questions do that job instead.

  1. Can you show me proof of liability insurance? A pressure washer can crack a window or strip paint. You want to know the company can pay for a mistake.
  2. Can you write down the price for each surface? Driveway, walk, patio and house listed separately makes quotes easy to compare.
  3. Will the siding, roof and any wood be soft washed rather than blasted? If the answer is no, keep looking.
