Why Houston needs more cleaning than most cities

Growth in Houston never gets a season off. The Gulf humidity keeps shaded concrete, north walls and fences damp for days after a rain, and mildew and algae grow year-round rather than just in summer. Spring adds a coat of pine and oak pollen, and every tropical storm leaves a film of mud and debris behind. That is why so many Houston homes and businesses clean on a schedule rather than waiting until something looks bad.

Pressure washing and soft washing are different jobs

The single most important thing to understand before hiring anyone: pressure is the right tool for hard, flat surfaces and the wrong one for almost everything else.

SurfaceMethod
Driveways, sidewalks, patiosPressure, with a surface cleaner for an even finish
House walls, brick, siding, stuccoSoft wash: low pressure and a cleaning solution
Shingle roofsSoft wash only. Pressure strips the granules
Wood fences and decksLow pressure with a wood cleaner, along the grain
Oil and rust stainsTreated with the right chemistry first, then rinsed

A crew that uses one high-pressure setting for everything will leave a clean-looking driveway and a damaged house. Ask how each surface will be cleaned.

After a storm or flood

Mud and debris left by a tropical storm can usually be washed off concrete and siding once it is safe. Floodwater is different: it can carry sewage and chemicals, so anything that sat in it should be cleaned with that in mind, and anything soaked inside the house is a job for water-damage restoration rather than a pressure washer.

Common questions

What is the difference between pressure washing and power washing?

In everyday use, nothing meaningful. Technically, power washing uses heated water, which helps with grease and oil. The more important distinction is pressure washing versus soft washing.

How often should a Houston home be washed?

Most homes do well with a yearly wash, with shaded concrete and north-facing walls sometimes needing more. Houston's humidity means growth comes back faster than in drier cities.

Can pressure washing damage my house?

Yes, if high pressure is used on walls, windows, roofs or wood. Those surfaces should be soft washed at low pressure with a cleaning solution.

Do I need to be home?

Usually not, as long as the crew can reach an outdoor water spigot and the areas to be cleaned. Close windows and move cars and furniture first.
