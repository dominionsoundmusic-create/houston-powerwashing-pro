Katy is two different cleaning jobs, depending on where you are

In the master-planned sections — Cinco Ranch, Cross Creek Ranch, Firethorne and the rest — the exterior cleaning conversation is usually about deed restrictions. The association inspects, a letter arrives, and the driveway or the siding needs attention by a date. Straightforward work on relatively new construction.

Closer to the reservoirs it is a different conversation. Properties in and around the Barker and Addicks flood pools carry a history that shows on the exterior: silt lines on brick and fence posts, a grey tidemark on garage walls, mud pressed into the texture of a concrete driveway. That is not algae and it does not respond to the same treatment.

Silt and flood staining

Flood residue is mineral rather than biological. It sits in the pores of the surface instead of growing on top of it, which changes the approach in two ways.

  • It usually needs more dwell time, not more pressure. The cleaning solution has to work into the surface before anything is rinsed.
  • It may not come out completely. On porous brick, a deep silt line can leave a shadow. Anyone who promises a perfect result on flood staining without looking at it first has not looked at it.

If your property took water, say so when you call. It changes the quote, and a crew that has done post-flood work will ask about it before you mention it.

New construction has its own residue

Katy is still building, and a house in its first year often carries things a five-year-old house does not: mortar splash on brick, paint overspray, adhesive marks on concrete, a driveway stained by whatever was parked on it during the build. These are builder's mess rather than weathering, and several respond to specific treatments rather than a general wash. Point them out rather than assuming they will lift.

The HOA letter, if that is what brought you here

The deadline mechanics are the same across the Houston master-planned communities, and they are covered in full on the Cypress page. The short version for Katy: read the date rather than the tone, and tell the association the work is booked.

What to ask before you book

  • Have you worked on flood-affected properties? Only relevant near the reservoirs, but very relevant there.
  • Soft wash or pressure on the house? Soft wash. Every time.
  • Does the quote include the walkway and entry? This is where cheap quotes hide their price.
  • What do you do about landscaping? Beds need wetting before and rinsing after, or you lose plants.
