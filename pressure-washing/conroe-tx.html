Lakefront property is a different job from a subdivision house

If your property is on or near Lake Conroe, you have surfaces most Houston-area homes do not: a dock, a boathouse, a bulkhead, steps down to the water, maybe a covered slip. They weather faster than anything on the house, because they are wet more often and shaded by their own structure.

Dock boards are the usual reason people call. A dock that has gone black and slick is not dirty, it is growing something, and by the time it is noticeably slippery it has been building for a season or two. That is a safety job as much as an appearance one.

Cleaning over water means the runoff goes into the lake. Ask what solution is being used and whether it is appropriate for that. A crew that works the lake regularly will already have an answer; one that does not may not have thought about it.

Wood needs less pressure than people expect

Most dock boards, deck boards and older fencing here are wood, and wood is the surface most often ruined by enthusiastic cleaning. Too much pressure raises the grain — the soft summer growth between the rings tears out, leaving a furry surface that looks clean for a month and then greys faster than before, because the roughened fibres hold water.

  • Low pressure and the right solution cleans wood without opening the grain.
  • If you are staining or sealing afterwards, the wood has to dry properly first — usually a couple of days in this climate, longer if it has been humid.
  • A dock that is already furry from a previous bad clean may need sanding rather than washing.

Pines, big lots and long driveways

Conroe lots run larger than the Houston suburbs and driveways run longer, which matters for quoting: a driveway here can be several times the area of one in a master-planned subdivision, and a per-square-foot price behaves very differently from a flat rate. Get the number in square feet rather than in adjectives.

The tree canopy does the same thing it does in Spring — needles and leaves in gutters and roof valleys, shade keeping surfaces damp, algae on the north side of everything.

What to ask before you book

  • Have you cleaned docks before? Working over water is its own skill and not every crew does it.
  • What pressure are you using on the wood? Low. If the answer is a number that sounds impressive, keep calling.
  • How is the driveway priced? Square footage, on a large lot.
  • How long before I can stain? A crew that says "straight away" has not thought about it.
